# Cross-cutting evidence: retail day-trader performance, finfluencer skill, prop-firm "funded trader" outcomes, regulatory record, and how trading track records are (or aren't) verified

Notes compiled 2026-10-14. Older material is dated inline. "WP" = working paper. All figures are attributed to the source that reports them. Where a figure comes only from secondary coverage or from an interested party, that is flagged.

---

## 1. Academic evidence on day-trader profitability and persistence (Taiwan, Brazil, US; options and 0DTE; finfluencers; course participants)

### Takeaway
The most complete datasets (all day traders in an entire market) agree on three points. Most day traders lose money net of costs. A very small minority (under 1% in Taiwan) earns persistent, predictable profits. Most people keep trading despite losses. Brazil's futures data puts the share who could "earn a living" at about 1%. US retail options and 0DTE traders lose money on average, largely to spreads. On social platforms, the least skilled finfluencers attract the most followers.

### Cited Findings

**Taiwan: Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (BLLO), complete exchange data 1992–2006**
- "The Cross-Section of Speculator Skill: Evidence from Day Trading" (WP May 2011; published *Journal of Financial Markets* vol. 18, 2014). About 360,000 Taiwanese individuals day trade in an average year. About 15% of them earn abnormal returns net of fees (commissions plus transaction taxes) in that year. The authors note that "some outperformance would be expected by sheer luck." — [BLLO 2011 WP PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- In an average year only about 1,000 day traders ("less than 1 percent of the total population of day traders") predictably earn reliably positive abnormal returns net of trading costs in the following year. These are the top performers from the prior year. — [BLLO 2011 WP](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf); [published abstract, IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v18y2014icp1-24.html)
- The top 500 day traders, ranked on prior-year returns, earn 49.5 bps per day before fees and 28.1 bps after fees in the next year (2011 WP). Traders with a history of losses earn −17.5 bps before fees and −34.2 bps after fees. The published 2014 abstract gives 61.3 (37.9) bps per day for the top 500, so the figures differ by paper version. — [BLLO 2011 WP](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf); [IDEAS abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v18y2014icp1-24.html)
- The profitable few are not liquidity providers. They place aggressive (immediacy-demanding) orders, and their profits are not concentrated around earnings announcements. — [BLLO 2011 WP](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- BLLO (2009), using Taiwan data for 1995–1999, find that individual investors' aggregate trading losses exceed 2% of Taiwan's annual GDP. — cited in [BLLO 2011 WP](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- "Do Day Traders Rationally Learn About Their Ability?" (Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean & Zhang, Oct 2017 version; later circulated as "Learning, Fast or Slow"):
  - The aggregate net-of-fee performance of day traders "is negative in each of the 15 years that we study."
  - More than 75% of day traders quit within two years.
  - For traders with at least 10 days of day trading, Kaplan-Meier survival is 44% at 1 year, 24% at 2 years and 15% at 3 years.
  - Traders with 10+ days of experience and positive lifetime profits are consistently about 5% of day traders.
  - Previously unprofitable traders account for 72% of day-trading volume over the sample and about 80% in the final years.
  - Unprofitable traders with 50+ days of experience have a 95.3% probability of day trading again within 12 months. Profitable ones have 96.4%. The authors call this "remarkable trading persistence in the face of losses."
  - Source: [BLLZ Oct 2017 WP PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf); [2018 "Learning fast or slow" version](https://www.econ.puc-rio.br/uploads/adm/seminarios/Learning_fast_or_slow_180606.pdf)
- About 140,000 individuals day trade in the average month, "just shy of 1% of the adult population." Day trading is about 17–20% of Taiwan Stock Exchange volume. — [BLLZ 2017 WP](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf); [BLLO 2011 WP](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)

**Brazil: Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?" (FGV WP no. 525; SSRN 3423101; c. 2019–2020)**
- Sample: all 19,646 individuals who began day trading Bovespa mini-index futures in 2013–2015. Only 1,551 (7.9%) persisted for more than 300 days. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101); [FGV repository](https://repositorio.fgv.br/items/e87d04fc-4b4c-4a56-ab5a-1c84c1afde1b/full)
- 97% of those who persisted more than 300 days lost money. Only 17 people (1.1% of the 1,551) earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage (about US$16/day). Only 0.5% earned more than a bank teller's starting salary (about US$54/day); the SSRN abstract says 0.5% and the FGV/EconPapers versions say 0.4%. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fgv:eesptd:525); [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/day-trading-a-bust/)
- The authors report no evidence of learning by day trading. They frame the result against course providers' claims: "it is virtually impossible for individuals to day trade for a living, contrary to what course providers claim." The data came from Brazil's securities regulator (CVM). — [FGV repository](https://repositorio.fgv.br/items/e87d04fc-4b4c-4a56-ab5a-1c84c1afde1b/full); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/?p=4771)
- Results are net of exchange and brokerage fees but before income taxes and course or platform costs. Including those costs would make the results worse. CXO Advisory cautions that the sample may not represent other countries or assets. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/day-trading-a-bust/)

**United States: stocks, options and 0DTE**
- Jordan & Diltz (Douglas J. Jordan and J. David Diltz), "The Profitability of Day Traders," *Financial Analysts Journal* 59(6), Nov 2003. US day traders in 1998–1999 (sample size unverified; one secondary site says 334 accounts):
  - Losing traders outnumbered profitable ones by roughly 2 to 1.
  - About 1 in 5 was more than marginally profitable.
  - Profitability tracked moves in the Nasdaq Composite.
  - Source: [CFA Institute abstract](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2003/the-profitability-of-day-traders); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v59y2003i6p85-94.html)
- Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya, "Retail Trading in Options and the Rise of the Big Three Wholesalers," *Journal of Finance* 78(6), 2023:
  - Retail trading "recently reached over 60% of total market volume" in US options.
  - Nearly 90% of payment for order flow comes from three wholesalers.
  - Retail investors "prefer cheaper, weekly options with average bid-ask spread of 12.6%, and lose money on average."
  - Source: [JF article via LBS Research Online](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827)
- Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda, "Retail Traders Love 0DTE Options… But Should They?" (SSRN 4404704, March 2023; CBOE SPX data Jan 2021–Feb 2023). Figures as reported by Larry Swedroe, May 23, 2023:
  - Retail lost more than $70 million over the sample.
  - Average daily losses were $184,000 over the full sample and $358,000 after daily expirations began on May 16, 2022.
  - About 60% of losses came from transaction costs and about 60% from 0DTE puts.
  - About 75% of retail S&P 500 option trades were 0DTE.
  - Source: [Wealth Management (Swedroe)](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options); [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/retail-0dte-option-trader-performance)
  - Caveat: these figures were not checked against the SSRN text, which was inaccessible.
- de Silva, Smith & So, "Losing is Optional: Retail Option Trading and Expected Announcement Volatility" (WP 2022; listed by Stanford GSB as *Review of Finance* 2025):
  - Retail investors concentrate option buying before earnings announcements.
  - They lose 5–9% on average around announcements and 10–14% around high expected-volatility announcements.
  - Losses are compounded by wide spreads and by holding options after volatility collapses.
  - Source: [Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/losing-optional-retail-option-trading-expected-announcement); [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2023/02/19/the-wealth-destroying-behavior-of-retail-option-trading)
- European regulators found that 74–89% of retail CFD accounts typically lose money, with average losses per client of €1,600–€29,000. This was the basis for ESMA's 2018 leverage caps of 30:1 to 2:1. — [ESMA press release, March 2018](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933)

**Finfluencers: skill of social-media stock pickers**
- Kakhbod, Kazempour, Livdan & Schürhoff, "Finfluencers" (Swiss Finance Institute WP 23-30, April 2023; later versions 2024–2025; AEA 2025 program). StockTwits data covering more than 29,000 finfluencers and 72 million tweets:
  - About 28% are skilled, earning about +2.6% monthly abnormal returns.
  - About 16% are unskilled.
  - About 56% are "antiskilled," earning −2.3% monthly abnormal returns. Some versions give 55% and 17%.
  - Source: [SFI WP page](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/n-23-30-finfluencers); [AEA 2025](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2025/program/paper/i3ry3n4t); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/what-can-we-extract-from-the-financial-influencers-advice/)
- Antiskilled and unskilled finfluencers have more followers, more activity and more influence on retail trading than skilled ones. Betting against their advice earns about 1.2% per month out of sample. — [SFI news release](https://www.sfi.ch/en/about-us/news/finfluencers-under-the-microscope-advice-vs.-entertainment); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/what-can-we-extract-from-the-financial-influencers-advice/)
- Skill classifications persist between the two halves of the sample, but the autocorrelation of raw alpha estimates is close to zero. In other words, a finfluencer's recent raw performance says little about the next period. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/?p=30437)
- The SFI release recommends transparency, active quality tracking and verification of finfluencer advice. — [SFI news](https://www.sfi.ch/en/about-us/news/finfluencers-under-the-microscope-advice-vs.-entertainment)

**Trading-course participants**
- The only outcome data found for customers of a specific US trading-education firm is regulatory, not academic. The FTC alleged that for Warrior Trading customers (2018–2021) "the vast majority of customer accounts actually lost money," some by thousands of dollars on top of course fees. Warrior settled without admitting or denying (see section 3). — [FTC press release, Apr 19, 2022](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/04/federal-trade-commission-cracks-down-warrior-trading-misleading-consumers-false-investment-promises)

### Inferences
- The datasets differ in country, instrument and era but point the same way: a small right tail of persistently profitable day traders, below 1% in Taiwan and about 1% in Brazil earning above minimum wage, inside a large population that loses in aggregate.
- A famous trader may genuinely be in that tail. Even so, the base rate for a person adopting the same style is set by the whole population, not by the tail.
- The Taiwan result that about 15% of day traders are profitable in a single year, against fewer than 1% who are predictably profitable, shows how one good year (or one good screenshot) overstates skill. Many one-year winners are lucky.
- Retail options and 0DTE losses are driven largely by spreads and transaction costs. Copying an influencer's option-heavy strategy adds a structural cost that the influencer's best-case examples may not show.
- The finfluencer evidence suggests that audience size is, if anything, negatively related to skill on StockTwits. Popularity is not evidence of skill.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that tracks outcomes for paying students of a named trading course (US or elsewhere). The only data point is the FTC's allegation about Warrior Trading customers, which was settled without admission.
- The Jordan & Diltz full text (exact sample size) and the Beckmeyer et al. primary text could not be accessed. Their figures rest on abstracts and secondary summaries.
- No direct study was found of followers of specific YouTube or Discord educators of the type named (e.g., ICT, TJR, Qullamaggie). The finfluencer research covers StockTwits posters, not paid-course communities.
- No study was found on 2024–2026 retail 0DTE outcomes after the Beckmeyer et al. sample. Later work that may disagree (e.g., exchange-sponsored analyses) was not searched.

## 2. Prop-firm / "funded trader" evidence: pass rates, payout rates, the My Forex Funds case, and why payout certificates are weak evidence

### Takeaway
Few firms publish full funnel data. The data that does exist (industry-vendor statistics, interested-party CEO statements, one firm's financial statements) suggests that roughly 5–20% of evaluations are passed and only about 1–7% of accounts ever receive a payout, with typical payouts small relative to account size. The CFTC's 2023 fraud case against My Forex Funds alleged that the firm was counterparty to its customers' trades. That case was dismissed with prejudice in May 2025, with sanctions imposed on the CFTC for misconduct, so the allegations were never adjudicated on the merits.

### Cited Findings
- **FPFX Tech data** (back-office provider to prop firms; shared with Finance Magnates in 2024):
  - Only 7% of 300,000 prop-trading accounts achieved a payout.
  - The average payout was about 4% of the funded plan size.
  - The average challenge fee was about $100.
  - Source: [Finance Magnates, Mar 18, 2025](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/only-1-in-20-traders-pass-prop-firm-challenges-reports-the-funded-trader/)
  - The same FPFX data are cited for an average pass rate of 14%, with about half of those passing reaching a payout. — [TradeInformer, Mar 7, 2025](https://www.tradeinformer.com/prop-weekly/what-ftmo-tells-us-about-prop-firm-finances)
- **The Funded Trader** (interested party; CEO Angelo Ciaramello on Discord, March 2025):
  - The challenge pass rate is 5–10%.
  - About 20% of funded traders receive payouts.
  - The overall chance of a payout is about 1–2%.
  - The firm had a backlog of 1,272 traders awaiting funds and settled with some traders at 10–30% of amounts owed.
  - Source: [Finance Magnates, Mar 18, 2025](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/only-1-in-20-traders-pass-prop-firm-challenges-reports-the-funded-trader/)
- **PipFarm** told Finance Magnates that 41% of its clients reach the payout stage. No sample was disclosed and the figure is an outlier. — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/only-1-in-20-traders-pass-prop-firm-challenges-reports-the-funded-trader/)
- **FTMO** (one of the largest firms) reported 2023 revenue of $203.9M, "operational costs" of $100.3M and staff costs of $4.7M. It does not disclose pass rates or total trader payouts. A $100k challenge cost €540. — [TradeInformer](https://www.tradeinformer.com/prop-weekly/what-ftmo-tells-us-about-prop-firm-finances)
  - A guide says FTMO, Apex and most firms publish no pass-rate disclosure. Third-party FTMO estimates of about 10–12% are labeled estimates. — [Traders Second Brain](https://traderssecondbrain.com/guides/prop-firm-pass-rate)
- **Topstep** 2025 figures, attributed to "Topstep's official 2025 data" by a third-party vendor page (not verified against a Topstep publication, no denominators given):
  - 16.8% of Trading Combines were completed into Express Funded accounts.
  - 33.3% of funded traders received a payout.
  - 0.71% of all starters reached a Live Funded account.
  - Source: [Tradecovex, Apr 2026](https://tradecovex.com/guides/how-many-combines-passed-express-topstep-2025)
- Apex's first-attempt pass rate is estimated by a third party at 15–20%, rising to about 40% with resets. This is unverified and not firm-disclosed. — [QuantVPS](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/prop-firm-statistics-2025)
- **CFTC v. Traders Global Group ("My Forex Funds"), D.N.J.:**
  - **Allegations.** The CFTC announced the fraud complaint on Sep 1, 2023 (the court acted Aug 29, 2023). It alleged fraudulent solicitation and that Traders Global itself, not a third-party "liquidity provider," was the counterparty to substantially all customer trades. — [CFTC release 8771-23](https://cftc.gov/node/247111)
  - **Figures.** Secondary reporting of the complaint: more than 135,000 customers paid more than $310M in fees since Nov 2021. — [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/cftc-sues-alleged-bucket-shop-my-forex-funds-after-310-million-prop-trading-fraud/)
  - **Ontario regulator.** The parallel Ontario Securities Commission application said "for the vast majority of investors, trading is simulated." Defendants argued that no customer invested money that was at risk. — [Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/from-the-regulators/osc-seeks-cease-trade-in-forex-fraud-case/)
  - **Outcome.** On May 13, 2025, Judge Edward S. Kiel dismissed the case with prejudice and sanctioned the CFTC. This followed Special Master Jose L. Linares's finding that the CFTC made "false representations" and acted "willfully and in bad faith." The issue was a CAD 31.5M transfer, cited to justify the asset freeze, that was reportedly a tax payment to the Canada Revenue Agency. — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/my-forex-funds-parent-defeats-cftc-in-court-as-judge-imposes-sanctions/); [Willkie Compliance Concourse](https://complianceconcourse.willkie.com/articles/new-jersey-federal-judge-dismisses-forex-case-with-prejudice-and-awards-attorney-fees/); [Quinn Emanuel](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/our-notable-victories/quinn-emanuel-wins-dismissal-of-cftc-case/)
  - **Sanction amount.** One source says more than $3M in fees under Rule 11. This is unverified. — [Day Trading Toolkit](https://daytradingtoolkit.com/market-insights/prop-firm-regulation-funded-traders-guide)
  - **Afterward.** MFF reportedly began sending payout emails in April 2026. This is a third-party, unofficial report. — [FundedTrading.com](https://fundedtrading.com/what-happened-to-my-forex-funds/); [Finance Magnates on comeback](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/myforexfunds-hints-at-comeback-after-winning-legal-battle-against-cftc/)
- Many prop firms argue that simulated-account products fall outside CFTC jurisdiction. That position is contested; the source is commentary. — [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/prop-firms/prop-firm-regulatory-status)

### Inferences
- **Why a payout certificate is weak evidence of skill:**
  - (a) Funnel statistics imply that with thousands of challenge buyers, chance alone will produce many people who pass and get paid at least once. A single certificate is consistent with luck. This is the same problem as Taiwan's "15% profitable this year" against "<1% predictably profitable."
  - (b) Average payouts are small: about 4% of nominal account size per FPFX. A certificate showing a large nominal account does not show large or repeated gains.
  - (c) Certificates are issued by the firm. They are not audited, show neither the losses on failed challenges nor reset fees, and usually involve simulated rather than real-market fills (per the OSC description of MFF).
  - (d) A trader can run many challenges at once or in sequence and show only the winners. Without the full count of attempts, the denominator is unknown.
  - (e) A firm's business model can depend on challenge fees more than on trading profits. A certificate then reflects the firm's payout policy as well as the trader's performance.
- The My Forex Funds outcome shows that the prop-firm model's legal status in the US is unresolved. Dismissal with prejudice for agency misconduct is not a ruling that the business practices alleged were lawful.

### Gaps
- No prop firm was found that publishes audited, cohort-level funnel data (attempts → pass → first payout → repeat payouts → lifetime net of fees). The Topstep figures could not be traced to a primary Topstep document.
- No data was found on how many funded traders achieve repeated payouts over 12+ months, which is the persistence measure analogous to BLLO's.
- No primary-source confirmation was found of the exact sanction amount in the MFF case, or of whether the CFTC appealed.
- No Bloomberg, WSJ, FT or Reuters primary reporting on 2024–2026 prop-firm industry economics was retrieved. Trade press (Finance Magnates, TradeInformer) was used instead.

## 3. Regulatory actions against trading educators and influencers (FTC, SEC, CFTC, FINRA, FCA), and earnings-claim rules

### Takeaway
The clearest US precedent for trading educators is FTC enforcement of earnings claims: Warrior Trading (2022, $3M), Raging Bull (2022, $2.425M) and Online Trading Academy (2020). These cases establish that advertising an instructor's own results as representative, without evidence of typical customer results, is treated as a deceptive earnings claim. SEC and DOJ cases against finfluencers center on "scalping" or pump-and-dump. The flagship 2022 Atlas Trading case was dismissed and then reinstated on appeal in October 2025. The FTC proposed in January 2025 to extend earnings-claim rules to investment and coaching "money-making opportunities." Its final status was not confirmed.

### Cited Findings

**FTC v. Warrior Trading, Inc. and Ross Cameron (W.D. Mass., announced April 19, 2022)**
- **Allegations.** Warrior Trading made "tens of millions of dollars" from 2018 to 2021 selling day-trading programs. Its ads showcased CEO Ross Cameron's trading results and called his strategies "profitable" and "scalable." Example ad copy: "Learn How I Made over $101,280.47 in Verified Profits Day Trading Part Time in Under 45 Days." Cameron allegedly said he guaranteed that "next week, the week after, the week after that" he would be trading stocks that move up 20–30%. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/04/federal-trade-commission-cracks-down-warrior-trading-misleading-consumers-false-investment-promises)
- **Customer outcomes.** The FTC alleged "the vast majority of customer accounts actually lost money." Consumer-facing FTC material says the advertised results were not typical and the company lacked evidence for its claims. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/04/federal-trade-commission-cracks-down-warrior-trading-misleading-consumers-false-investment-promises); [FTC consumer alert](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2022/04/day-trading-earnings-werent-payday-warrior-trading-promised); [Complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2023198WarriorTradingComplaint_0.pdf)
- **Settlement.** A stipulated order approved by a 4–0 Commission vote:
  - Warrior Trading pays $3M for consumer refunds.
  - It is barred from unsubstantiated earnings claims.
  - It may not misrepresent that buyers can succeed regardless of education, capital or time spent trading.
  - It must comply with the Telemarketing Sales Rule.
  - Warrior neither admitted nor denied the allegations.
  - Source: [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/04/federal-trade-commission-cracks-down-warrior-trading-misleading-consumers-false-investment-promises); [Final order PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Warrior%20Trading%20Final%20Order.pdf)
- **Refunds.** In January 2023 the FTC sent more than $2.9M to 20,402 people. — [FTC, Jan 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/01/ftc-returns-more-29-million-consumers-harmed-warrior-trading); [Refunds page](https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/refunds/warrior-trading-refunds)

**Other FTC trading-education cases**
- **FTC v. RagingBull.com** (sued Dec 2020; settlement 2022). Settling defendants including Jason Bond and Jeff Bishop agreed to pay $2.425M. The order bars earnings claims without written substantiation and requires express consent and easy cancellation for recurring subscriptions. The case against instructor Kyle Dennis continued. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/antitrust/investment-site-to-pay-ftc-2-4-million-for-earnings-deception)
- **FTC v. Online Trading Academy** (settled Sept 2020). OTA must forgive debt for thousands of consumers. Principals pay $5M–$9.1M and turn over assets. Earnings claims are restricted unless substantiated. — [FTC press release, Sept 2020](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2020/09/ftc-settlement-requires-online-trading-academy-forgive-consumer-debt-principals-turn-over-millions)

**FTC earnings-claim rulemaking**
- November 2022: the FTC issued an advance notice exploring expansion of the Business Opportunity Rule. — [FTC, Nov 2022](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/11/ftc-explores-changes-possible-expansion-its-business-opportunity-rule)
- January 2025: the FTC issued a proposed rule to extend the Business Opportunity Rule to "money-making opportunities" such as business coaching and investment opportunities.
  - The rule would be renamed the "Business and Money-Making Opportunity Rule."
  - It would apply misrepresentation bans plus recordkeeping and substantiation requirements, and would define "earnings."
  - The vote was 3–2; Commissioners Ferguson and Holyoak dissented.
  - Source: [FTC NPRM PDF](https://ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/r511993bizoppnprm01132025.pdf); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ftc-puts-pedal-to-the-metal-to-release-proposed-rule-on-mlm-earnings-claims-changes-to-business-opportunity-rule-but-republican-commissioners-pump-the-brakes-in-potential-foreshadowing)
  - Status as of Oct 2026 was not confirmed (see Gaps).

**SEC / DOJ: finfluencers**
- **SEC v. Constantinescu et al. (S.D. Tex.; SEC release 2022-221, Dec 14, 2022).**
  - **Allegations.** Eight men, seven of whom presented themselves as successful traders on Twitter and in the "Atlas Trading" Discord, allegedly bought stocks, urged followers to buy, and sold without disclosing their plans to sell. The SEC put profits at about $100M; DOJ's parallel indictment says at least $114M (Jan 2020–Apr 2022). An eighth defendant (podcaster Daniel Knight) was charged with aiding and abetting. — [SEC press release 2022-221](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2022-221); [DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/eight-men-indicted-114-million-securities-fraud-scheme-orchestrated-through-social-media)
  - **Criminal case.** The district court dismissed the indictment, reasoning that it relied on a "right-to-control" theory barred by *Ciminelli v. United States* (2023). On Oct 2, 2025 the Fifth Circuit reversed in *United States v. Constantinescu*, No. 24-20143. Relying on *Kousisis* (2025), it held that the object of the scheme was obtaining money from followers, and reinstated the indictment. — [5th Cir. opinion](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/24/24-20143-CV0.pdf); [CLS Blue Sky (Quinn Emanuel)](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2025/10/16/quinn-emanuel-discusses-fifth-circuit-decision-reinstating-securities-fraud-indictment)
  - **Status.** No later trial outcome was found. The allegations are unproven.
- **SEC v. Michael M. Beck** ("@BigMoneyMike6," up to about 3M followers; conduct 2017–2019). The SEC alleged he made about $870,000 by "scalping," that is, recommending stocks he was selling. — [Wealth Management](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/node/142114)
- **SEC v. Andrew Left and Citron Capital (C.D. Cal., July 26, 2024).**
  - The SEC alleges a roughly $20M scheme in which Left publicly recommended long or short positions at least 26 times in 23 companies, then quickly reversed his own positions.
  - It also alleges Citron misrepresented itself as independent of hedge-fund compensation.
  - DOJ brought a parallel indictment; Left pleaded not guilty on July 29, 2024.
  - Source: [SEC Litigation Release LR-26056](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26056); [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/financial-services-law-advisor/2024/07/sec-doj-charge-citron-and-left-with-illegal-trades)
  - No outcome was found.

**FINRA, SEC Marketing Rule and UK FCA**
- **FINRA v. M1 Finance (March 2024).** This was FINRA's first formal finfluencer enforcement action. M1 was fined $850,000 because about 1,700 paid influencers (Jan 2020–Apr 2023), who generated more than 39,400 new accounts, posted content that was not fair or balanced or was promissory or misleading (FINRA Rules 2210 and 2010). — [ThinkAdvisor](https://thinkadvisor.com/2024/03/18/finra-levies-first-enforcement-action-in-finfluencers-sweep); [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/?p=250948)
- **SEC Marketing Rule sweep (Sept 11, 2023).** Nine registered investment advisers settled charges for advertising hypothetical performance on public websites without the required policies and procedures. Combined penalties were $850,000 ($50k–$175k each). The rule's compliance date was Nov 4, 2022. — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2023/09/sec-marketing-rule-enforcement-charges-against-nine-registered-investment-advisers); [Simpson Thacher](https://stblaw.com/about-us/publications/view/2023/09/12/sec-marketing-rule-settlements-total-10-before-one-year-anniversary-of-compliance-date)
- **UK FCA (May/June 2024).** The FCA charged nine "finfluencers" over an unauthorised CFD/forex trading scheme promoted on Instagram (2018–2021) to combined audiences of about 4.5M. It was the FCA's first prosecution of influencers for unauthorised financial promotions. Pleas were not guilty except one, and trial is set for February–March 2027. — [FCA press release](https://fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/finfluencers-charged-promoting-unauthorised-trading-scheme); [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/uk-finfluencer-trial-date-set-crown-court-2027)

### Inferences
- Across the FTC cases, the regulatory standard is that an educator's personal P&L, or selected student testimonials, used to imply what buyers will earn must be backed by evidence of typical customer results. An instructor's genuine personal profitability does not make such marketing lawful.
- The SEC and DOJ finfluencer cases concern undisclosed selling into followers' buying (scalping or pump-and-dump), not strategy quality. Followers of real-time trade "callouts" face a front-running and conflict risk that exists separately from whether the strategy works.
- Settlements are typically "neither admit nor deny." The allegations in these cases should be described as allegations.

### Gaps
- Whether the FTC's Business and Money-Making Opportunity Rule has been finalized, withdrawn or left pending as of Oct 2026 could not be confirmed.
- Outcomes after Oct 2025 in *Constantinescu* (trial, plea, cert petition) and in the SEC/DOJ case against Andrew Left were not found.
- No CFTC action specifically against a trading educator or influencer in 2024–2026 was found in these searches. Searches were not exhaustive.
- Regulatory histories of the specific individuals named in the assignment (other than the FTC's Warrior Trading/Ross Cameron action) were not searched here. Individual-level records were left to other researchers.
- The court docket confirming final entry of the Raging Bull order was not retrieved; Bloomberg Law describes a proposed settlement.

## 4. What counts as credible verification of a trader's record, and the selection and survivorship biases that apply even to verified winners

### Takeaway
Verification standards form a hierarchy. Registered-adviser regimes (CTA disclosure documents, the SEC Marketing Rule) impose net-of-fee, actual-results and hypothetical-performance rules. GIPS verification is firm-wide process assurance, not an audit of any specific track record. Real-money contests like the US Investing Championship (USIC) check brokerage statements but are opt-in leaderboards with many entrants, single-year windows and voluntary monthly listing. Even genuinely verified winners are drawn from a large field where most participants lose: only 8% of USIC entrants were profitable at 10 months in 2022.

### Cited Findings

**CFTC / NFA rules for commodity trading advisors (CTAs)**
- A CTA's disclosure document cannot include hypothetical performance if the trading program has three months of actual trading results. Proprietary (own-account) results under CFTC Reg. 4.35(a)(8) must be clearly labeled, placed last, and pro-forma adjusted for client fees. — [NFA FAQ: CTA Disclosure Documents](https://www.nfa.futures.org/faqs/members/cta-disclosure-documents.html)
- NFA members presenting hypothetical performance must use the NFA Compliance Rule 2-29(c)(1) cautionary disclaimer. — [CFTC Letter 01-60](https://cftc.gov/tm/letters/01letters/tm01-60.htm)
- Amended NFA Rule 2-29 requires advertised performance to be net of all commissions, fees and expenses. — [Akin Gump, CFTC/NFA year-end update](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/cftc-and-nfa-year-end-regulatory-updates)

**SEC Marketing Rule (investment advisers)**
- Hypothetical performance requires policies and procedures. In Sept 2023, nine advisers were penalized for posting it without them. — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2023/09/sec-marketing-rule-enforcement-charges-against-nine-registered-investment-advisers)

**GIPS**
- A GIPS verification "does not confirm the accuracy of performance results presented in any specific composite presentation."
- A separate, optional "performance examination" attests to a specific composite.
- Verification does not "certify or guarantee" compliance.
- Source: [CFA Institute Market Integrity blog](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/marketintegrity/2011/09/01/gips-standards-enhancing-verification-guidance/); [KMCO](https://www.kmco.com/?p=4508); [GIPS verifier guidance (asset owners)](https://www.gipsstandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2020-gips-standards-verifier-when-verifying-asset-owners.pdf)

**US Investing Championship (USIC): real-money, statement-verified contest**
- **Format.** Calendar-year, real-money, time-weighted percentage return. Verified by brokerage statements, plus live account walkthroughs at the organizer's discretion. — [CTE Research Sheet, Sept 2026 (third-party compilation)](https://completetradersedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CTE-Research-Sheet-US-Investing-Championship-2.pdf)
- **Structure.** Account bands are $20,000+ and $1,000,000+, each with a Stock division and an Enhanced Growth division (futures and long options), giving four boards. Entry fee is $475 or $1,000 and there is no cash prize. Monthly listing is optional. Scoring starts from the day a competitor joins. — [CTE Research Sheet](https://completetradersedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CTE-Research-Sheet-US-Investing-Championship-2.pdf)
- **Denominator data from the organizer:**
  - 1H 2022: 23 of 291 participants (8%) reported profits.
  - Ten months of 2022: 26 of 316 participants (8%) reported profits.
  - Full year 2022: 326 participants.
  - Source: [Business Wire, July 2022](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220725005328/en); [Business Wire, Nov 28, 2022](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20221128005230/en); [Business Wire, Jan 2023 final standings](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230125005300/en)
  - 1H 2026: 115 of 688 (17%) reported profits while the S&P 500 gained 10.2%. — [CTE Research Sheet](https://completetradersedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CTE-Research-Sheet-US-Investing-Championship-2.pdf)
- **Documented results for traders in this space:**
  - Mark Minervini won the 1997 stock division at +155% on $250,000 of his own money. This rests on Minervini's and the organizer's publicity; no independent copy of the 1997 statements was found.
  - Minervini won the 2021 $1M+ stock division at +334.8%. — [Kyodo/press release, Jan 2022](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202201256538); [CTE Research Sheet](https://completetradersedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CTE-Research-Sheet-US-Investing-Championship-2.pdf)
  - Oliver Kell won the 2020 $20k+ Stock division at +941.1%.
  - Martin Luk won the 2025 $20k+ Stock division at +969.8%.
  - Bob Weissman (listed with "Minervini Private Access") won the 2025 $1M+ Enhanced Growth division at +115.4%.
  - Source for these three: [CTE Research Sheet](https://completetradersedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CTE-Research-Sheet-US-Investing-Championship-2.pdf)
- **Path vs. destination.** Interim leaders often vanish from the voluntary monthly boards; for example, a 2026 Enhanced Growth leader at +1,678.8% after three months was absent from later boards. Final figures are often far below peaks: Leos Mikulka in 2024 went from +960.4% in November to a final +409.6%. — [CTE Research Sheet](https://completetradersedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CTE-Research-Sheet-US-Investing-Championship-2.pdf)
- **Contest history.** The original contest (1983 to mid-1990s) paused amid an SEC investigation and restarted in 2019. A 1998 SEC proceeding (Release 33-7560) found that organizer Norman Zadeh's Prime Advisors sold unregistered hedge-fund securities and that Zadeh failed to keep required books and records; civil penalties included $165,000 for Zadeh. Callers responding to published contest results were reportedly solicited into the partnerships. The compiler summarizes: "It is a verified leaderboard, not an audited composite or an allocatable track record." — [CTE Research Sheet (citing SEC Release 33-7560)](https://completetradersedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CTE-Research-Sheet-US-Investing-Championship-2.pdf)

**Luck versus skill in large fields**
- In Taiwan about 15% of day traders are profitable in a given year, but fewer than 1% are predictably profitable the next. — [BLLO 2011 WP](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- Among finfluencers, the autocorrelation of raw alpha is near zero. — [Quantpedia on Kakhbod et al.](https://quantpedia.com/?p=30437)

### Inferences
**Practical hierarchy of evidence, strongest to weakest:**
1. Multi-year, audited or regulator-governed records: a CTA disclosure document with net-of-fee actual results, or a GIPS-compliant composite with a composite-specific examination. Even these do not prove future skill.
2. Multi-year real-money contest results verified against brokerage statements (USIC), read alongside the field's base rate.
3. Third-party broker-linked trackers (e.g., Myfxbook "verified," Kinfo), whose verification depth was not established here.
4. Self-published broker statements or screenshots.
5. Prop-firm payout certificates and "simulated/small-account challenges."
6. Unverified claims.

**Biases that apply even to verified winners:**
- *Multiple-entrant selection.* With hundreds of USIC entrants each year (and hundreds of thousands of day traders), some extreme returns are expected by chance. Leverage, concentration and options in the Enhanced Growth division widen that distribution.
- *Short windows.* Single calendar-year returns, partly time-weighted from a late entry date, reward variance.
- *Opt-in and voluntary reporting.* Losers can drop off the monthly boards, and people who never enter are invisible.
- *Survivorship in media.* Books, podcasts and courses feature winners. "Market Wizards"-style compilations are a list of survivors, with no denominator.
- *Time-weighted vs. dollar-weighted returns.* A small account compounded at 900% is not the same as large-dollar profits, and educators may later trade very different sizes.
- *Regime dependence.* Momentum and breakout strategies can show outsized single-year results in strong markets; the 8% profitable share in the 2022 bear market against 17% in 1H 2026 illustrates this.

The most informative signal of skill is persistence across many independent periods, ideally with the full count of attempts disclosed. That is the measure BLLO use.

### Gaps
- No authoritative description was found of what Myfxbook "Track Record Verified"/"Trading Privileges Verified" or Kinfo verification actually checks, or of their known failure modes (e.g., selective account linking, multiple accounts). No sources were retrieved, so no claims are made.
- USIC participant counts for most years come from the organizer's releases and a third-party compiler (CTE, Sept 2026). The share of all entrants profitable in full-year results is not published for most years.
- Independent verification of the 1990s USIC results (including Minervini's 1997 result) was not found beyond organizer and participant publicity.
- No academic study of US Investing Championship winners' subsequent performance was found.

## 5. Strategy decay after publication and data snooping in technical trading rules

### Takeaway
Published anomalies lose roughly half their return after publication. McLean & Pontiff (2016) report returns 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower post-publication. Once data snooping and transaction costs are accounted for, the best historical technical trading rules have not delivered out of sample: Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999) and Bajgrowicz & Scaillet (2012). This implies that a widely publicized "famous trader" strategy, especially one optimized on hindsight charts, should be expected to underperform its showcased history.

### Cited Findings
- **McLean & Pontiff, "Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?" *Journal of Finance* 71(1): 5–32, Feb 2016.**
  - Across 97 published cross-sectional return predictors, returns are 26% lower out of sample (an upper bound on data-mining effects) and 58% lower after publication.
  - The extra decline of about 32 points is attributed to investors trading on the published findings.
  - An earlier draft with 95 predictors reported 25% and 56%.
  - Source: [AFA, JF vol. 71 issue 1](https://afajof.org/issue/volume-71-issue-1/); [CFA Institute digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2016/06/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-digest-summary); [published PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)
- **Sullivan, Timmermann & White, "Data-Snooping, Technical Trading Rule Performance, and the Bootstrap," *Journal of Finance* 54(5): 1647–1691, 1999** (LSE FMG DP 303, Oct 1998):
  - The authors expand Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron's 26 rules to a universe of 7,846 rules and apply White's Reality Check on about 100 years of daily DJIA data. — [FMG DP 303 PDF](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-11/dp303.pdf); [FMG abstract page](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/publications/discussion-papers/data-snooping-technical-trading-rule-performance-and-bootstrap)
  - In-sample (1897–1986), certain rules outperform even after the data-snooping adjustment.
  - Out of sample (1987–1996, 3,291 days), the best rule selected as of end-1986 (a 5-day moving average) produced a mean return of 2.8% with nominal p = 0.322.
  - The probability that the best-performing rule did not beat the benchmark is "nearly 12 percent," so there is "scant evidence that technical trading rules were of any economic value during the period 1987–1996."
  - S&P 500 futures data, where transaction costs are easier to control, likewise show no rule outperforming.
  - Source: [FMG DP 303](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-11/dp303.pdf)
- **Bajgrowicz & Scaillet, "Technical trading revisited: False discoveries, persistence tests, and transaction costs," *Journal of Financial Economics* 106(3): 473–491, 2012.**
  - Using DJIA 1897–2011 and false-discovery-rate controls, they find an investor "would never have been able to select ex ante the future best-performing rules."
  - Apparent outperformance is "completely offset by the introduction of low transaction costs."
  - The authors question the economic value of technical rules reported for early periods.
  - Source: [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v106y2012i3p473-491.html); [CFA Institute digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2013/02/technical-trading-revisited-false-discoveries-persistence-tests-and-transaction-costs-digest)
- **Costs are a first-order drag for active retail styles:**
  - About 60% of retail 0DTE losses came from transaction costs. — [Wealth Management](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options)
  - Retail options spreads average 12.6%. — [Bryzgalova et al. 2023](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827)
  - In Taiwan, top day traders must clear a 30 bps tax on sales plus commissions. — [BLLO 2011 WP](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)

### Inferences
**How this applies to copying famous traders:**
- (a) Discretionary chart-pattern methods taught with hand-picked historical examples (e.g., "liquidity sweeps," "order blocks," breakout setups) face a version of the data-snooping problem: the examples are chosen after the fact from many possible charts and many possible rules.
- (b) Once a method is broadcast to large audiences, any real edge from crowded entry points may shrink. McLean & Pontiff's post-publication decay is the closest academic analogue, though it concerns cross-sectional anomalies rather than discretionary intraday methods.
- (c) Rules that look profitable before costs can vanish after realistic spreads, slippage and fees. This matters most for high-turnover intraday and options strategies.
- (d) Even the authors who find genuine in-sample outperformance show that the best rule could not be identified in advance. The investor's problem is choosing which famous trader or rule to follow before the fact, not after.

These are reasoned applications of the cited studies, not direct tests of any named educator's method.

### Gaps
- No academic study was found that directly tests ICT-style (order blocks, fair-value gaps, liquidity sweeps) or similar retail-popular discretionary concepts out of sample. The applicability of the technical-rule literature to those methods is by analogy.
- Studies on post-publication decay of momentum and breakout strategies taught by specific practitioners (e.g., the Minervini SEPA/VCP or Qullamaggie-style breakouts) were not searched. Momentum-factor literature specific to those styles may exist and was outside this note's searches.
- The published JF 1999 text of Sullivan, Timmermann & White was not separately checked. Figures are from the October 1998 FMG discussion-paper version marked "Journal of Finance, Forthcoming."
