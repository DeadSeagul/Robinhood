# Momentum Swing Trading: Qullamaggie, Minervini, and Kell (Rules, Track Records, Independent Evidence)

Research date: 2026-10-14. Labels used throughout: **[CLAIM]** = the trader's own statement or his publisher's/marketing copy; **[3P]** = third-party verification or third-party summary; **[INF]** = my inference. Several primary pages (Business Wire, TraderLion, jotup mirror) returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool. Where a finding comes only from a search-result summary of such a page, the note says so.

## 1. Kristjan Kullamägi ("Qullamaggie"): mechanical rules for breakouts, episodic pivots, and parabolic shorts, plus track-record evidence

### Takeaway
His own blog post (Jan 8, 2021; updated Apr 28, 2021) gives unusually specific rules: top 1–2% momentum universe, a prior 30–100%+ move, a 2-week to 2-month tightening consolidation that rides the 10/20-day MAs, entry at the opening-range high (1/5/60-min), a low-of-day stop no wider than ADR, a 1/3–1/2 partial sale after 3–5 days, and a trail on the first close below the 10- or 20-day MA. His results ($5k in 2011 to a ~$100M+ peak in 2021) are self-reported. The only third-party review reported so far is that Jack Schwager and George Coyle say they saw his statements for *Market Wizards: The Next Generation* (Harriman House, June 2026). Nothing has been published as an audit.

### Cited Findings
**General risk and sizing [CLAIM]**
- Risk per trade is usually 0.25–1% of the account and rarely above 1%. With smaller accounts (under a few million) he risked about 0.5–1.5%. Typical positions are 10–20% of the account, and he never holds more than 30% of the account overnight in one stock. — [Qullamaggie blog, "3 timeless setups"](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- He says a 25–30% win rate can be very profitable if losses stay small, and he cites a personal database of "thousands" of setups built over 7–8 years. — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- The book-derived version is stricter: risk 0.5% or less per trade, with 1% as an absolute maximum. Skip the trade if the low-of-day stop is wider than one average daily true range, and never widen the stop. He uses margin about half the time when trading goes well and has no hard daily loss limit. — [Complete Trader's Edge profile (L. van Riet, Aug 16, 2026), summarizing *Market Wizards: The Next Generation* ch. 1](https://completetradersedge.com/?p=233509)
- Market filter (book-derived): take long setups only when the index's 10-day MA is above its 20-day MA and both are rising. A 10/20 bearish cross is a caution signal. — [CTE profile](https://completetradersedge.com/?p=233509). A blog commenter (Feb 2023) gives the same reading for the S&P 500: 10-day SMA above 20-day SMA, both rising, usually about a week after the cross. That comment is not the author's own text. — [Qullamaggie blog comments](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)

**Setup 1: Breakout (long) [CLAIM]**
- Universe: the top 1–2% of stocks by performance over 1, 3 and 6 months. — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- Prior move: 30–100%+ within the past 1–3 months, built over a few days to a few weeks. — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- Consolidation: 2 weeks to 2 months, with higher lows and a tightening range, while price "surfs" the rising 10- and 20-day MAs (sometimes the 50-day). — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/). The book-derived summary instead says "about one to three weeks". This discrepancy matters for parameter choice. — [CTE profile](https://completetradersedge.com/?p=233509)
- Entry: the opening-range high of the first 1-minute, 5-minute or 60-minute candle. Daily-chart breakout entries are also described. The book-derived version adds 30-minute bar highs. — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/); [CTE profile](https://completetradersedge.com/?p=233509)
- Stop: the low of the day, never wider than the stock's ADR/ATR. Example: with a 5% ADR, the stop must be no more than 5% below entry. — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- Exits: sell 1/3 to 1/2 after 3–5 days and move the stop to breakeven. Trail the rest with the 10- or 20-day MA, exiting on the first close below it; beginners should use the 10-day. — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/). The choice of 10 versus 20 depends on how extended the stock is. — [CTE profile](https://completetradersedge.com/?p=233509)
- Claimed payoff: 10–20x+ the initial risk on winners in a bullish market. — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- The post gives no numeric volume multiple for breakouts, no ADR/ATR lookback, and no liquidity (price or dollar-volume) floor. — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/); [CTE profile](https://completetradersedge.com/?p=233509)

**ADR% filter [3P, not primary]**
- A community TradingView indicator says it follows his TC2000 setup. It defines ADR% as the 20-day average percentage daily range and calls a stock a "momentum leader" at roughly 5–6%+ ADR%. — [TradingView "Qullamaggie Daily with ADR and Compression Range"](https://www.tradingview.com/script/ZBI3bJ2W-Qullamaggie-Daily-with-ADR-and-Compression-Range)
- StockCharts forum users note that ADR excludes gaps while ATR includes them, and that he uses ADR as a percentage. One user scanned for ADR 3–7% at prices of $30–200 with high volume; that is the user's choice, not his. — [StockCharts ADR scan thread](https://scan.stockcharts.com/discussion/2489/adr-scan)

**Setup 2: Episodic Pivot (EP) [CLAIM]**
- A gap up of 10%+ on news, most often earnings or guidance. Volume must be heavy pre-market or in the first 5–30 minutes; the best cases trade their full average daily volume within 15–30 minutes. — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- Preferred fundamentals: mid-, high- or triple-digit EPS and revenue growth and a significant beat. "Neglect": the stock should not have rallied strongly over the prior 3–6 months. — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- Entry is the opening-range high (1/5/60-min) with a low-of-day stop. After that, trail with the 10/20-day MA; the post also mentions 20/50 for longer holds. He says mastering the setup takes 4–8 earnings seasons. — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- Book-derived scan: stocks up at least 10% on high volume each day. Entry above the first 5-min bar high, or above the first 1-hour bar if confirmation takes longer. — [CTE profile](https://completetradersedge.com/?p=233509)

**Setup 3: Parabolic short, and its mirror long [CLAIM]**
- Short when large caps are up 50–100%+, or small caps 300–1000%+, over a few days to weeks, with 3–5+ consecutive up days. Entry: the opening-range low (1/5-min), the first red 5-min candle, or a failed bounce into VWAP. Stop: the high of day, or a reclaim of VWAP. Targets: the 10- and 20-day MAs. Claimed reward is about 5–10x risk. — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- Book-derived version: the stock must be up 3–4 consecutive days and at least 300% in total. Never short on day 1 and rarely on day 2. — [CTE profile](https://completetradersedge.com/?p=233509)
- Parabolic long: after a parabolic short falls 50–60%+ in a few days, buy the opening-range high, the first green 5-min candle or a range break, and expect a 50–100% bounce over a few days. — [Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)

### Inferences
- [INF] Daily-bar backtest spec for breakouts:
  - Universe: rank by 21/63/126-day return and keep the top 1–2% on any horizon.
  - Prior move: require a ≥30% gain from a low to a high within the prior 21–63 bars.
  - Consolidation: 10–40 bars with rising swing lows and contracting range (e.g., the 5-day ATR falling relative to the 20-day ATR), price closing above rising 10/20 SMAs, and ADR%(20) of at least about 4–5%.
  - Trigger: a break above the consolidation high.
  - Stop: the entry-day low, capped at 1×ADR%.
  - Exits: sell 1/3–1/2 on day 3–5 with the stop raised to breakeven; exit the rest on the first close below the 10-SMA (variant: 20-SMA).
  - Market filter: the index 10-SMA above the 20-SMA, both rising.
- [INF] The opening-range entry and low-of-day stop need intraday data. Daily bars cannot tell whether the ORH was hit before the eventual low of day, so use 5-min bars: buy-stop at the first 5-min bar high, stop at the session low so far, and reject entries where (entry − LOD)/entry > ADR%. A daily-bar approximation that assumes the entry filled and the stop is the day's low will overstate results on reversal days.
- [INF] EP spec: an earnings or news gap of ≥10% (open versus prior close), first-30-min volume ≥ 1× the 20-day average daily volume, and a prior 63–126-day return below some threshold for "neglect". The 10%-gap and volume rules are mechanical; the "significant beat" fundamental needs point-in-time estimates data.
- [INF] Two of the three setups are long-only momentum and the reported gains are concentrated in 2020–2021, so any backtest should report results by regime. A 25–30% win rate with fat right tails makes results very sensitive to a few outliers and to survivorship in the universe (see Section 4).

### Gaps
- I could not access the book text. The exact book wording on consolidation length, MA conditions and verification method is known only through CTE's secondary summary.
- I found no primary source for his liquidity filter (minimum price, dollar volume or float) or the exact ADR% formula and threshold. The 5–6% ADR figure comes from a third-party script.
- I found no independent, published audit or brokerage-statement image from him (Twitter screenshots were not retrievable). The Swedish-tax "certification" claim is unverified.

## 2. Mark Minervini: SEPA / Trend Template, VCP, entries, stops, exits, and championship results

### Takeaway
The Trend Template is a set of mechanical stage-2 filters on 50/150/200-day SMAs, the 52-week low and high, and RS rank. It is easy to code. The VCP (successive, shrinking pullbacks with volume dry-up, bought on a pivot breakout) is looser and must be operationalized. His stated stop rule is a 10% absolute maximum with a 6–7% average loss; the 7–8% figure in the brief is not his stated rule in the sources found. His 1997 USIC win (+155% on $250k) is attested by his own materials, his publisher and a 2021 organizer release. His 2021 USIC win (+334.8%, $1M+ Stock division) is in the organizer's release. The 220%/yr 1994–99 figure comes from Schwager's interview and his own materials and has never been audited.

### Cited Findings
**Trend Template (secondary sources quoting his book *Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard*, 2013) [CLAIM as reported by 3P]**
- Criteria as listed by ActionAlerts (Jan 1, 2023):
  1. Price above the 50-, 150- and 200-day MAs.
  2. 150-day MA above the 200-day.
  3. 200-day MA trending up for at least 1 month, preferably 4–5 months.
  4. 50-day MA above both the 150- and 200-day.
  5. Price at least 30% above its 52-week low, preferably 100–300%+.
  6. Price within 25% of its 52-week high, the closer the better.
  7. IBD relative strength rank no less than 70, preferably 80s–90s.

  The page condenses the usual 8 points into 7 by merging "price > 50MA" into point 1. — [ActionAlerts Substack](https://actionalerts.substack.com/p/using-mark-minervinis-trend-template); same 30% / 25% wording at [EdgeRater template](https://edgerater.com/tradingtemplates/georgelea_markminervini)
- Coding variants: some TradingView scripts use 25% rather than 30% above the 52-week low. Some drop the RS requirement or replace IBD's proprietary RS with outperformance against SPY. — [TradingView Minervini Trend Template script](https://www.tradingview.com/script/22JWROvE-Mark-Minervini-s-Trend-Template); [TradingView LevelUp screener](https://it.tradingview.com/script/xt1TZYqW-LevelUp-Minervini-Trend-Template-Screener)
- In 2018, Colin Twiggs (Incredible Charts) reported that he "could not get an exact fit" when screening for the template. — [Incredible Charts](https://tradingdiary.incrediblecharts.com/2018-10-29-whats_new.php)

**VCP, entry, stops, exits [3P summaries]**
- Base shape: typically 2–4 contractions, occasionally 5–6, each shallower than the last (e.g., about 20% → 10% → 5%). Volume dries up to the lowest levels of the base in the final contraction. Pivot = the high of the final tight area. Entry = a breakout above the pivot on expanding volume. Stop = below the last contraction's low. — [LuxAlgo VCP concept page](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/volatility-contraction-pattern/)
- A Financial Wisdom summary of *Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard* (Apr 30, 2020; updated Dec 7, 2025) gives:
  - Buy only in Stage 2; avoid Stage 3 and 4.
  - Base 3–65 weeks; volume contracts into the pivot with at least one very-low-volume day.
  - Buy above the pivot on expanding volume.
  - Absolute maximum loss 10%, targeting a 6–7% average loss.
  - Raise the stop once the stock is up 2–3x the initial risk.
  - Hold 4–6 stocks (10–12 in large portfolios).

  — [Financial Wisdom: Mark Minervini](https://www.financialwisdomtv.com/post/mark-minervini)

### Inferences
- [INF] Daily-bar Trend Template spec:
  - close > SMA50 > SMA150 > SMA200
  - SMA200 today > SMA200 21 bars ago (strict version: 84–105 bars)
  - close ≥ 1.30 × the 252-day low and close ≥ 0.75 × the 252-day high
  - RS proxy: the 252-day return (or IBD-style weighted 63/126/189/252-day return) ranked at ≥70th percentile of the universe
- [INF] VCP operationalization (one option):
  - Inside a Trend-Template stock, find a base of 15–65 bars.
  - Identify ≥2 successive swing-high-to-swing-low pullbacks, each with smaller percentage depth than the prior (e.g., each ≤ 0.6× the prior).
  - The final contraction's depth is ≤ about 10%, and its 5-day average volume is < 50–75% of the 50-day average.
  - Pivot = the high of the final contraction. Enter on a close (or intraday print) above the pivot with volume ≥ 1.4–1.5× average; the volume threshold is a commonly cited heuristic and is not sourced here.
  - Stop = min(last contraction low, entry × 0.90).

  Results will be highly sensitive to these choices.
- [INF] The USIC figures are real-money, statement-checked, one-year, time-weighted returns. They are not a multi-year audited composite. The 1994–99 220% figure has no independent audit in the public record I found.

- I found no source for a "7–8% max stop" attributed to Minervini. That number is usually associated with O'Neil's CAN SLIM, but I did not source that attribution here.

## 3. Oliver Kell: "cycle of price action" with 10/20 EMAs, and the 2020 USIC result

### Takeaway
Kell's cycle (reversal extension → wedge pop → EMA crossback → base-n-break → exhaustion extension → wedge drop → downside crossback) is defined relative to the 10- and 20-period EMAs. The descriptions are qualitative; third-party indicators supply numeric thresholds (e.g., extension = bar low more than 1.5×ATR above the 10 EMA). His 2020 USIC result, +941.1% in the $20k+ (under $1M) Stock division, appears in the organizer's year-end release and in independent compilations. It was a record for that board until Martin Luk's +969.8% in 2025.

### Cited Findings
**Rules [3P summaries; I could not access Kell's e-book or book text]**
- Reversal extension: a capitulation at the cycle bottom, on heavy volume, where the lower timeframe becomes extended and then snaps back into the MAs. Wedge pop: the first rally back up through the moving averages after that extension, breaking short-term resistance and reclaiming the MAs; this is the entry. EMA crossback: the next pullback into the MAs, where one waits for supportive buying. Base n' break: a shorter consolidation into or above the MAs after a strong move, then a breakout from that range. — [TraderLion, "Cycle of Price Action"](https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/cycle-of-price-action-by-oliver-kell/) (403 to fetch; content via search summary); [TraderLion, Base n' Break](https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/base-n-break-how-to-catch-breakouts/)
- Wedge drop: the first move down through the MAs after an upside extension. Downside EMA crossback: the first rally back into the declining MAs after a wedge drop, which is a short entry. — [TraderLion, Downside EMA Crossback](https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/downside-ema-crossback-sell-short-into-resistance/); [TradingView "Wedge Pop/Drop" (QuantVue)](https://www.tradingview.com/script/GOkJ7o5J-Wedge-Pop-Drop-QuantVue)
- Numeric extension proxy (indicator default, not Kell's own number): an upside extension is a bar whose low is more than 1.5×ATR above the 10 EMA. — [TradingView QuantVue Wedge Pop/Drop](https://www.tradingview.com/script/GOkJ7o5J-Wedge-Pop-Drop-QuantVue). Another script measures exhaustion as percentage distance from the 10 EMA plus volume climaxes, uses a 10/20 EMA stack for bias, and gives no published thresholds. — [TradingView "Oliver Kell Cycle of Price Action"](https://vn.tradingview.com/script/xHyBTcMq-Oliver-Kell-Cycle-of-Price-Action)
- Financial Wisdom's video summary of his e-book method (Aug 5, 2021; updated Nov 17, 2025) covers the following. It notes he "does not divulge the exact detail" of some trades.
  - Indicators: 10/20 EMAs on all timeframes, plus the 50/200-day SMAs.
  - Entries: buy as price breaks through the 20-day EMA after a reversal, buy a break above a daily consolidation supported by the 10/20 EMAs, and buy 10-EMA bounces after a breakout.
  - Stops: just under the 10/20-day EMA support (or the 20-week EMA on weekly pullbacks), raised under the MAs as the trade advances.
  - Exits: sell into a "blowoff exhaustion/extension" far above the 10-day EMA that reverses on volume, and exit on a large bearish candle closing through both the 10 and 20 EMAs.
  - Sizing: 8–12 positions, with maximum allocations of 35% ×2, 20% ×2, 12% ×4 and 8% ×4. These caps total 190% of equity, so up to about 2:1 margin.

  — [Financial Wisdom: Oliver Kell](https://www.financialwisdomtv.com/post/oliver-kell-us-investing-champion)

### Inferences
- [INF] Daily-bar backtest spec for Kell's long cycle:
  - Reversal extension: close < EMA10 − k×ATR(14) (k ≈ 1.5–2) with volume > 1.5× the 50-day average.
  - Wedge pop: within N≈10 bars after the reversal extension, close back above both EMA10 and EMA20 and above the high of the prior 3–5-bar range.
  - EMA crossback: after a wedge pop, the low touches EMA10/EMA20 while the close stays above EMA20; enter above that bar's high.
  - Base-n-break: a 5–15-bar range above EMA20 with contracting ATR, entered on a close above the range high.
  - Stop: below EMA20 (or the entry-bar low).
  - Profit-taking: partial when the low is more than k×ATR above EMA10.
  - Final exit: a close below both EMA10 and EMA20 (wedge drop).

  All thresholds here are my parameterizations, not Kell's.
- [INF] The 2020 result came in a single year in which large-cap growth and "stay-at-home" stocks rallied sharply after March 2020, and it was won with up to about 2:1 margin (per the sizing summary). It shows a real, statement-checked one-year outcome, not a durable edge.

### Gaps
- I could not read *Victory in Stock Trading* or Kell's e-book directly, and TraderLion pages were blocked to direct fetch. The wording of each phase rests on secondary summaries.
- I found no public USIC results for Kell in 2021 or later. CTE's podium-level tables do not list him, but monthly listing is voluntary, so non-appearance is not evidence of anything.

## 4. Independent evidence on these setup types; what the USIC verifies; independent backtests

### Takeaway
The building blocks have strong academic support: 3–12-month cross-sectional momentum, time-series momentum, nearness to the 52-week high, and post-earnings drift. That support is weaker for the specific discretionary patterns (VCP, Qullamaggie breakouts, Kell's cycle), for which I found no peer-reviewed or rigorous independent backtests. PEAD has decayed toward zero in recent US data, and momentum has crash risk and cost drag. The USIC is a real-money, brokerage-statement-checked, one-year leaderboard with a large unprofitable denominator. It is not an audited multi-year track record.

### Cited Findings
**Cross-sectional momentum**
- Jegadeesh & Titman (1993, *JF*), NYSE/AMEX stocks, Jan 1965–Dec 1989:
  - Winner-minus-loser strategies earn significant returns over 3–12-month holds.
  - The best (12-month formation / 3-month hold) earned 1.31% per month, or 1.49% with a 1-week skip.
  - The 6-month formation earns about 1% per month regardless of holding period.
  - The 6/6 strategy has about 84.8% semiannual turnover and, after a 0.5% one-way cost, a risk-adjusted 9.29% per year.
  - Part of the first-year abnormal return dissipates over the following two years.

  — [Jegadeesh & Titman 1993 (PDF)](https://floridapsc.com/library/filings/2013/07457-2013/Support/Gulf's%20response%20to%20OPC's%201st%20POD,%20No.%2057/Jegadeesh%20and%20Titman%201993%20(140-167).pdf)
- Daniel & Moskowitz ("Momentum Crashes"): momentum crashes occur in "panic" states (after market declines, with high volatility) and coincide with market rebounds, because past losers carry high beta. A dynamic, volatility-scaled version roughly doubles the static strategy's alpha and Sharpe ratio. — [NBER w20439](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)
- Turnover and cost drag remain material for momentum. One thesis applies the Novy-Marx & Velikov (2015) buy/hold-spread cost mitigation; this is a student study, not peer-reviewed. — [Gothenburg thesis 2019](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/items/23f70255-c2d5-46a8-bc7b-265542988fd7)

**52-week high (relevant to the Trend Template's "within 25% of high" and to breakouts)**
- George & Hwang (2004, *JF* 59:2145–2176): nearness to the 52-week high explains much of momentum profits and "dominates and improves upon" past-return forecasts. Its forecasts do not reverse in the long run, suggesting short-term momentum and long-term reversal are separate phenomena. — [SUFE abstract page](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/361244). Sample 1963–2001. — [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/the-52-week-high-as-a-momentum-indicator-for-individual-stocks)
- Out-of-sample caveats: in international stock indexes, momentum and reversals co-exist. — [NAU](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/the-52-week-high-and-momentum-investing-in-international-stock-in/). The 52-week-high strategy is unprofitable for emerging-market indices and less profitable than momentum in developed-market indices. — [Griffith](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/items/41995ba4-ea62-565b-bc6a-9233b7978ae0)

**Time-series momentum (relevant to MA-trend filters and trailing exits)**
- Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012, *JFE* 104:228–250): significant time-series momentum in all 58 liquid futures instruments studied (equity indices, currencies, commodities, bonds). Returns persist 1–12 months and partially reverse later; a diversified portfolio performs best in extreme markets. — [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463)
- Critique (Kim, Tse & Wald): monthly alpha falls from 1.27% with volatility scaling to 0.41% without it, and the edge vanished in 2009–2013. — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/deconstructing-the-time-series-momentum-strategy/)

**Earnings and news gaps (relevant to Episodic Pivots)**
- Bernard & Thomas (1989): the top-minus-bottom SUE decile spread was positive in 41 of 48 quarters. — [Wikipedia: PEAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post%E2%80%93earnings-announcement_drift). An event-study resource gives about a 4.2% spread over 60 trading days; this is secondary. — [EventStudyTools](https://www.eventstudytools.com/earnings-announcements)
- Kettell, McInnis & Zhao (UT Austin, Apr 2022 working paper): SUE-sorted hedge returns declined significantly from 1974 to 2020 and are "indistinguishable from zero" after 2017. They attribute much of the decline to falling persistence of earnings news, not only to arbitrage. — [Kettell et al. 2022 (PDF)](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/CEASA/Events%20Page/PEAD_Declined_over_time.pdf)
- Brandt, Kishore, Santa-Clara & Venkatachalam ("Earnings Announcements Are Full of Surprises"): the 3-day earnings-announcement return (EAR), which corresponds to a price-reaction gap rather than an accounting surprise, also predicts drift. Quantpedia cites about 12.5% per year for EAR/SUE strategies, and secondary theses disagree on whether EAR or SUE drift is stronger. — [Quantpedia PEAD](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/post-earnings-announcement-effect/)
- News versus no-news moves:
  - Chan (2003, *JFE* 70:223–260): drift after public news (strongest after bad news, weaker after good news) and reversal after extreme moves without news, mainly in smaller, illiquid stocks. — [SUFE abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/357702); [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/28248)
  - Jiang, Li & Wang (SSRN, 2018), intraday: news-driven returns continue without reversal, while non-news returns reverse. — [Villanova-hosted SSRN 2667658](https://www1.villanova.edu/dam/villanova/VSB/assets/marc/marc2018/SSRN-id2667658.pdf)
  - Contradiction: Frank & Sanati (*JFE* 2018) find positive news shocks are followed by reversal and negative ones by drift. — [UMN Experts](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/how-does-the-stock-market-absorb-shocks/)

**Breakout and technical rules; data snooping**
- Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999, *JF*) applied White's Reality Check to Brock–Lakonishok–LeBaron's 26 rules (MA and trading-range breaks) on about 100 years of DJIA data. BLL's results were not explained by data snooping in the original sample, but profitability disappeared in the subsequent 10-year sample. — [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/119144); [secondary summary (PDF)](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/216/Technical%20Analysis%20with%20a%20Long%20Term%20Perspective%20Trading%20Strategies%20and%20Market%20Timing%20Ability.pdf). On 1987–1996 DJIA data the best BLL rule earned an insignificant ~8.63% per year before costs. — [AgEcon Search](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/19039/files/cp05pa01.pdf)

**Survivorship and delisting bias (critical for single-stock momentum backtests)**
- Shumway (1997, *JF*): CRSP omits most negative-reason delisting returns since 1962, and the omitted returns are large. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v52y1997i1p327-40.html). Shumway & Warther (1999): applying −55% to missing performance delistings removes the apparent Nasdaq size effect entirely. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v54y1999i6p2361-2379.html)

**Independent backtests of VCP / Trend Template / Qullamaggie (none rigorous found)**
- TradingView: there are "Pure Minervini 10% TP / 5% CL" and "Qullamaggie Breakout V2" scripts. Neither publishes performance metrics, and both authors say further tuning or verification is needed. — [TradingView Pure Minervini](https://it.tradingview.com/script/H9HxZ9Zc-Pure-Mark-Minervini-10-TP-5-CL); [TradingView Qullamaggie Breakout V2](https://www.tradingview.com/script/5bTajWQM-Qullamaggie-Breakout-V2); [LuxAlgo Q Breakout V2](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/5bTajWQM-qullamaggie-breakout-v2/)
- A promotional TradingQnA post (Oct 11, 2026; Indian NSE/BSE data, 2000–2026) claims:
  - a Trend-Template-filtered VCP model took 0 trades in 2008;
  - bases with undercutting later lows failed more than 62% of the time;
  - a "non-undercutting floor" filter raised profit factor from 1.12 to 1.526.

  It reports no trade count, no survivorship handling and no out-of-sample test. — [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/a-25-year-empirical-study-on-minervinis-volatility-contraction-pattern-vcp-what-2-35m-data-points-reveal-about-market-crashes/198732)
- Other VCP "tests" are anecdotal: 36 of a hand-picked top-100 stocks formed clean VCPs over 12 months, which is survivorship-biased by construction. — [Financial Wisdom](https://www.financialwisdomtv.com/post/can-one-chart-pattern-beat-the-market-i-tested-the-top-100-stocks). StockSharp's "VCP" demo (about 166% average annual return) is an intraday ATR/MA example, not a pattern test. — [StockSharp](https://doc.stocksharp.com/en/api-examples/0043_VCP)

**What the US Investing Championship verifies**
- Format at the 2019 restart: a calendar year of real money. Entrants name an account number at the start, brokerage statements for that account verify claims, the minimum account was $20,000, and the fee was $350 then. — [CMT Association / AP](https://cmtassociation.org/press-release/ap-us-investing-championship-to-restart-in-2019/)
- Current structure:
  - Time-weighted returns, verified by brokerage statements plus discretionary live account walkthroughs.
  - Four boards: $20k+ and $1M+ (Money Manager Verified Ratings) × Stock and Enhanced Growth (futures and long options allowed).
  - 2026 fees: $475 (USIC) / $1,000 (MMVR); no cash prize.
  - Monthly listing is optional; late entrants start from 0% at their entry date.

  CTE calls it "a verified leaderboard, not an audited composite or an allocatable track record". — [CTE Research Sheet](https://completetradersedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CTE-Research-Sheet-US-Investing-Championship-2.pdf)
- Denominators are the organizer's own figures: in 2022, after ten months, 26 of 316 participants (8%) were profitable; in the first half of 2026, 115 of 688 (17%) were profitable while the S&P 500 gained 10.2%. Fields were 124 in 2020, 338 in 2021, 579 in 2025 and 704 at seven months of 2026. Several interim leaders stopped appearing, and peak-to-final give-backs of 220–550 points occurred. — [CTE Research Sheet](https://completetradersedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CTE-Research-Sheet-US-Investing-Championship-2.pdf)
- Organizer history: founded 1983 by Dr. Norman Zadeh, paused in the mid-1990s amid an SEC investigation, and restarted in 2019. A 1998 SEC proceeding (Release 33-7560) found that Zadeh's Prime Advisors sold unregistered hedge-fund LP interests, and callers responding to the published contest results were solicited. — [CTE Research Sheet](https://completetradersedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CTE-Research-Sheet-US-Investing-Championship-2.pdf)

### Inferences
- [INF] The academic evidence supports the ingredients these traders combine: past 3–12-month winners (the top-1–2% scan and RS ≥70), proximity to 52-week highs (Trend Template point 6), trend persistence (MA stacks and MA-close trailing exits), and continuation after news-driven gaps (EPs). It does not validate the specific discretionary pattern filters, the opening-range entry, or the 10–20R payoff claims.
- [INF] Qullamaggie's high-ADR, top-1–2% stocks lie in the small and illiquid region where Chan (2003) finds the strongest news-related effects. That same region carries the highest costs, slippage and delisting risk. Backtests need point-in-time universes including delisted names (Shumway-type corrections), realistic slippage on ORH stop orders, and gap-through-stop handling.
- [INF] Selection effects: the three traders are known because they are extreme right-tail winners (USIC fields of 100–700, with only 8–17% profitable). Their outcomes cannot by themselves establish that the methods have positive expectancy for others. Multi-year, statement-verified persistence is the more informative signal: Minervini won in 1997 and 2021, while Kell and Qullamaggie each rest on one exceptional period.
- [INF] Given PEAD decay after about 2017 and momentum crash risk, any backtest should include 2018–2026 out-of-sample results and report performance in panic/rebound months separately (e.g., Apr 2020, Nov 2020, Jan 2021, 2022).

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed or rigorous independent backtest (with trade counts, survivorship-free universe and costs) of the VCP, the Minervini Trend Template as a whole, Qullamaggie breakouts or EPs, or Kell's cycle.
- I did not obtain primary text for Bernard & Thomas (1989) Table 1, Brandt et al. (2008) or George & Hwang's monthly return magnitudes. Their magnitudes above are from secondary summaries.
- I found no academic study specifically of "10%+ gap on earnings with first-30-minute volume ≥ ADV" (the EP definition). The closest evidence is the EAR-drift and news/no-news literature.
- I did not retrieve the official financial-competitions.com rules page directly. Current USIC rules are from CTE's September 2026 sheet and the 2018/19 AP release.
