# Audits and backtests shrink famous traders' edges

The nine traders teach three kinds of trade. Michael Huddleston (ICT) and TJR teach "smart money" liquidity-sweep reversals at the New York open. Ross Cameron and Andrew Aziz teach momentum in the opening session. Qullamaggie, Mark Minervini, Oliver Kell, Linda Raschke and Peter Brandt teach daily-chart momentum or classical-pattern swing trades. All nine share one skeleton: **a stop where the chart idea is proven wrong, small fixed risk (0.25–2% of the account where a number is stated), and profits that come from a minority of large winners**. The most codeable rules are Raschke's 1995 *Street Smarts* setups, the research version of the 5-minute opening-range breakout (ORB) that Aziz co-authored, Minervini's Trend Template and Qullamaggie's own blog rules, while ICT and TJR leave the target, bias, gap size, entry point and stop anchor to judgment. The track-record evidence ranges widely. At the top is a CPA examination of Cameron's broker-confirmed realized gains ($18.8M over 2017–2025, before fees), which sits alongside his firm's 2022 FTC settlement over earnings claims made to customers. Next come one-year, statement-checked contest wins for Minervini and Kell and book authors' reviews of statements for Brandt and Qullamaggie. At the bottom are self-reported figures for Aziz and TJR and no published record for Huddleston. Independent evidence supports the ingredients (3–12-month momentum, nearness to 52-week highs, trend persistence) but not the named patterns: formal tests of ICT rules find nothing or worse. The setup you already trade, the QQQ 5-minute ORB, has a real gross edge of about **+0.13R per trade** (R = the amount risked), but an independent replication found it disappears at roughly **2 cents per share of slippage**. Sixty sessions of 5-minute bars cannot confirm any of these edges, because a once-a-day setup yields about 60 trades per symbol against the roughly 1,000 needed to detect +0.13R. The intraday data is best used to check mechanics, fills and costs, while two years of daily bars can host Turtle Soup, inside-day/NR4, Holy Grail and Trend Template tests. On a $60 account the 1% risk these traders use is 60 cents, so none of their sizing rules can be followed with even one option contract.

## Nine methods share one skeleton: a structure stop, small risk, rare big winners

The nine methods disagree about what to trade but agree on how to lose. Each sets the initial stop at the price that would prove the setup wrong. For ICT and TJR that is beyond the swept high or low; for Cameron, the pullback low; for Aziz, a close back through VWAP; for Qullamaggie, the low of the entry day; for Minervini, the last contraction low; for Kell, the 10/20-day EMA; for Raschke, one tick past the day's low; and for Brandt, the breakout-day "last day" bar. Where a sizing rule is published, it is small. Qullamaggie usually risks **0.25–1% of the account per trade** ([Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)). Aziz teaches Alexander Elder's **2%-per-trade and 6%-per-month caps** ([BBT Session 4 deck](https://bearbulltraders.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Session-4.pdf)). Brandt's worked example risks **0.8% of capital to make 3.2%** ([Brandt, *Diary* pp. 49–50](https://peterlbrandt.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/3-Day-Trailing-Stop-Rule-3DTSR.pdf)).

The payoffs are lopsided. The research 5-minute ORB wins only **24% of the time** and still averages +0.13R ([Zarattini & Aziz 2023](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5983d931579fb366729580d8/t/643ed6765176b45506e41a01/1681839734183/SSRN-id4416622.pdf)). Qullamaggie says a 25–30% win rate can be very profitable if losses stay small. A review of Schwager's chapter on Brandt reports that about 10–15% of his trades produce all of his profits ([Smart-Lab review](https://smart-lab.ru/blog/reviews/889963.php)). The group's two high win-rate claims are both self-reported: Cameron's "accuracy of 69%" over 24,268 trades ([Warrior, Bull Flag](https://www.warriortrading.com/bull-flag-trading/)) and TJR's 64.29% win rate in 2026 ([SozAI transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/updated-day-trading-strategy-2026/)). Neither comes with a broker-confirmed average win and average loss.

The methods differ most in how much they leave to judgment. For ICT and TJR, no source fixes the swing length, the minimum displacement or gap size, the entry point within the gap, the stop anchor or the target. Even the "killzone" time windows vary by 30 to 90 minutes between sources ([LuxAlgo Killzones](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/killzones/)). Cameron's exits depend on Level 2, time and sales, and "extension bars." Brandt's pattern selection is his own judgment. Kell's numeric thresholds come only from third-party indicators, and Minervini gives no fixed number of contractions or volume multiple for the VCP. Raschke is the exception, since *Street Smarts* specifies its setups bar by bar.

## Intraday rules hinge on the first ninety minutes of New York trading

### ICT and TJR wait for a sweep, a structure break and a gap

Huddleston teaches mainly in long YouTube lectures, and his official site offers a free library and makes no performance claims ([theinnercircletrader.com](https://theinnercircletrader.com/)). The working definitions therefore come from third-party codifications. A **bullish fair value gap** exists when the third candle's low is above the first candle's high. The gap's midpoint is called "consequent encroachment," and a gap that price later closes through becomes an "inversion" FVG ([LuxAlgo FVG](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fair-value-gap/)). Build Alpha codes a **liquidity sweep** as a bar that trades below the lowest low of the prior N bars and closes back above it. Its version adds a minimum sweep depth of 0.25×ATR(14) and a minimum FVG size of 0.35×ATR(14), and enters at the next bar's open ([Build Alpha](https://www.buildalpha.com/backtest-ict-and-smc/)).

Clock windows are part of the method, including the **Silver Bullet hours of 03:00–04:00, 10:00–11:00 and 14:00–15:00 New York time**. Sources disagree on where the windows start and end, by 30 to 90 minutes ([LuxAlgo Killzones](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/killzones/)). They also disagree on minimum targets: LuxAlgo's indicator uses 40 ticks (10 NQ/ES points), while a fan channel says "at least five handles" ([LuxAlgo Silver Bullet](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/ICT-Silver-Bullet)). Third-party guides describe the "2022 model" as a sequence: set a daily bias, wait for a sweep, wait for a market-structure shift with displacement, enter on an FVG inside a killzone, and target the opposing liquidity. They disagree on whether the stop goes beyond the sweep wick or beyond the displacement swing ([AlgoKings](https://www.algokings.net/learn/ict-2022-model/)). No primary source gave an official ICT swing length, gap size, fill definition or risk per trade.

TJR's 2026 description is more specific. He frames the strategy as a filter that is "less about what trade to take and more about what trades it stops us from taking" ([SozAI transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/updated-day-trading-strategy-2026/)). He trades **ES and NQ futures at the New York open**. First he marks "draws on liquidity": the previous day's high and low, the Asia and London session extremes, and 1-hour and 4-hour highs and lows. He waits for either index to sweep one of them, then requires a **5-minute break of structure or 5-minute inverse FVG** in the opposite direction. ES and NQ must agree on the 5-minute chart; if they are not aligned by about 10:20 ET, he would "call it a freaking day." He then waits for a 5-minute pullback into equilibrium or an FVG. The entry comes on the 1-minute chart, after a counter-break followed by a break back in the trade direction. Entering on the 5-minute signal alone, he warns, "is going to cause you guys to get a horrible entry."

The stop goes above the second high (below the second low for longs). Targets are the next hourly, session or 4-hour levels, with partial exits and a breakeven stop after the first. His examples run from about 1:1 to 1:3.4. He gives **no risk percentage, daily loss limit or fixed reward-to-risk rule**, and takes no pre-market entries. In an earlier video he calls the approach "the ideation and the thought process" ([SozAI, Path to Profitability](https://sozai.app/transcript/path-to-profitability-tjr-strategy/)).

About half of this vocabulary can be computed from regular-hours QQQ/SPY bars. RTH prior-day highs and lows, the opening range, intraday swings, 3-bar gaps, 50% retracements and the 10:00–11:00 and 14:00–15:00 windows all come from 09:30–16:00 bars. SPY and QQQ can stand in for ES and NQ in TJR's alignment test. Asia and London ranges, the midnight open, the London Silver Bullet window and the 08:30 news reaction need overnight futures data. A 23-hour futures session also produces different prior-day highs and lows than RTH bars do.

### Cameron and Aziz buy opening momentum in very different stocks

Cameron's claimed edge sits in his stock filter more than in his chart pattern. He trades stocks **up at least 10% on the day, with relative volume of at least 5× the 14-day average, priced under $20 (sweet spot $2–8), with a float under 20 million shares and a fresh news catalyst** ([Warrior watch-list page](https://www.warriortrading.com/day-trading-watch-list-top-stocks-to-watch/)). In a 2020 video quoted in the FTC's complaint, he said "almost 98%" of about $3.3M in profits came from stocks with relative volume of 5 or higher ([FTC Complaint ¶42](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2023198WarriorTradingComplaint_0.pdf)). The thresholds have drifted: a page first written in 2014 accepts floats under 100M and relative volume of 2× ([Warrior, Momentum Day Trading Strategy](https://www.warriortrading.com/momentum-day-trading-strategy/)). A backtest therefore has to name which version it tests.

His Gap and Go is for gaps above 4% between 9:30 and 10:00: "I buy the high of the first 1-min candle... with a stop at the low of that candle or buy the Pre-Market highs" ([Warrior, Gap and Go](https://www.warriortrading.com/gap-go/)). His bull flag enters on "the first candle to make a new high after 2-3 red candles of pulling back." The stop goes at the pullback low, and he requires a 2:1 profit-to-loss ratio. The setup has failed if the pullback retraces more than 50%, comes on heavier volume, or breaks the 9 EMA or VWAP. He sells half at the first target, moves the stop to breakeven, and on 5-minute entries exits the rest on "the first red candle on the 5min chart" ([Warrior, Bull Flag](https://www.warriortrading.com/bull-flag-trading/)).

He trades **09:30–11:30** and uses only the 5-minute chart after 11:30. He caps structural stops near 20 cents and sizes in dollars, so a 20-cent stop with a $500 maximum risk means 2,500 shares ([Warrior, Momentum](https://www.warriortrading.com/momentum-day-trading-strategy/)). He stops after two red trades and keeps a maximum daily loss whose amount he does not publish ([Warrior, Day Trading Rules](https://www.warriortrading.com/day-trading-rules/)). His Level 2, time-and-sales and "extension bar" exits are discretionary.

Aziz teaches a menu of setups keyed to time of day. At the open he trades ORB, ABCD, bull flag and "fallen angel" setups. Around 10:00–10:30 he trades VWAP false breakouts, and from mid-morning on he trades VWAP trend, moving-average trend and reversal setups ([BBT Session 4 deck](https://bearbulltraders.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Session-4.pdf)).

His discretionary ORB requires an opening range "significantly smaller than the stock's Average True Range," measured on candle bodies rather than wicks. The stop is "a close below VWAP for the long positions," the target is "the next important technical level," and the rule applies to 5-, 15- and 30-minute ranges ([Goodreads quotes, *Advanced Techniques in Day Trading*](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/52463925-how-to-day-trade-for-a-living-a-beginner-s-guide-to-trading-tools-and-t)). His 2023 playbook limits the ORB to **mid-float stocks above $10, with "No low floats"** ([BBT 5-Min ORB deck](https://bearbulltraders.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/5-Min-ORB-Strategy-ORB.pdf)). His float table assigns large-float stocks "mostly Moving Average and Reversal" setups ([BBT figures PDF](https://bearbulltraders.com/docs/AndrewAziz-How_to_Day_Trade_for_a_Living_AUDIOBOOK-FIGS.pdf)), which makes his methods more compatible with mega-caps than Cameron's.

The ORB in Aziz's co-authored research is a different, fully mechanical rule. On QQQ 5-minute bars, it trades in the direction of the first 5-minute candle, entering at the second candle's open and skipping dojis. The stop goes at the first candle's opposite extreme, and the trade takes profit at 10R or exits at the close. Each trade risks 1%, leverage is capped at 4×, and the test assumes no slippage ([Zarattini & Aziz 2023](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5983d931579fb366729580d8/t/643ed6765176b45506e41a01/1681839734183/SSRN-id4416622.pdf)). This research ORB is essentially the setup you trade now. It is also the only intraday rule in this report with a published specification, a published result and independent replications.

## Swing traders publish the most codeable rules

### Qullamaggie, Minervini and Kell ride momentum leaders on the daily chart

Qullamaggie's January 2021 blog post is unusually concrete ([Qullamaggie blog](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)). His **breakout** setup scans the top 1–2% of stocks by 1-, 3- and 6-month performance and wants a prior move of 30–100% or more over 1–3 months. That should be followed by a 2-week-to-2-month consolidation with higher lows and a tightening range that "surfs" the rising 10- and 20-day moving averages. He buys the opening-range high of the first 1-, 5- or 60-minute candle and stops at the low of the day, but never wider than the stock's average daily range. After 3–5 days he sells a third to a half and moves the stop to breakeven, then trails the rest until the first close below the 10- or 20-day MA.

The **episodic pivot** is a gap of 10% or more on news, usually earnings. It needs heavy early volume, ideally a full day's average volume within 15–30 minutes, in a stock that had not rallied over the prior 3–6 months. Entry and stop are the same as for the breakout: the opening-range high and the low of the day. The **parabolic short** targets large caps up 50–100% or more (small caps 300–1,000% or more) after 3–5 straight up days. It enters on the opening-range low, the first red 5-minute candle or a failed VWAP bounce, with the stop at the high of the day and targets at the 10- and 20-day MAs.

A summary of his chapter in *Market Wizards: The Next Generation* adds three things ([Complete Trader's Edge profile](https://completetradersedge.com/?p=233509)). It gives a market filter: longs only when the index's 10-day MA is above its 20-day MA and both are rising. It gives a stricter 0.5% risk cap. And it describes a consolidation of "about one to three weeks," which conflicts with the blog's 2 weeks to 2 months and matters for parameter choice. His own text gives no volume multiple, no lookback for the average daily range and no liquidity floor.

Minervini's Trend Template is the easiest filter in this report to code. It requires all of the following: price above the 50-, 150- and 200-day MAs; the 150-day MA above the 200-day; the 200-day rising for at least a month; and the 50-day above both longer MAs. Price must also be at least 30% above its 52-week low and within 25% of its 52-week high, and the IBD relative-strength rank must be at least 70 ([ActionAlerts](https://actionalerts.substack.com/p/using-mark-minervinis-trend-template)).

His volatility contraction pattern is looser. It typically shows 2–4 pullbacks, each shallower than the last (for example 20%, then 10%, then 5%), with volume drying up in the last one. The buy is a breakout above the final tight area on expanding volume, with the stop below the last contraction ([LuxAlgo VCP](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/volatility-contraction-pattern/)). His stated loss rule is an **absolute maximum loss of 10%, targeting a 6–7% average loss**. He raises the stop once the stock is up 2–3× the initial risk and holds 4–6 positions ([Financial Wisdom](https://www.financialwisdomtv.com/post/mark-minervini)). The 7–8% stop often attributed to him did not appear in the sources found. All of this wording is secondary, because his book and site could not be fetched.

Kell's cycle is defined against the 10- and 20-period EMAs ([TraderLion](https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/cycle-of-price-action-by-oliver-kell/)). It begins with a capitulation "reversal extension." Then comes a "wedge pop" back up through the averages, which is the first entry. An "EMA crossback" pullback into the averages follows, then a "base n' break" from a short consolidation and an "exhaustion extension" to sell into. The cycle ends with a "wedge drop" and a downside crossback, which are used for shorts.

Stops sit just under the 10/20 EMA and trail up, and a large bearish candle closing through both EMAs triggers a full exit. He holds 8–12 positions with caps of 35%, 20%, 12% and 8% that sum to about 190% of equity, implying up to roughly 2:1 margin ([Financial Wisdom: Kell](https://www.financialwisdomtv.com/post/oliver-kell-us-investing-champion)). Numeric thresholds come only from third-party indicators. One defines an upside extension as a bar whose low is more than 1.5×ATR above the 10 EMA ([TradingView QuantVue](https://www.tradingview.com/script/GOkJ7o5J-Wedge-Pop-Drop-QuantVue)).

### Raschke and Brandt wrote rules a computer can follow

Raschke and Connors' *Street Smarts* (1995) specifies setups bar by bar. It is written for futures, with prices in ticks and the night session ignored ([Street Smarts text](https://vdoc.pub/documents/street-smarts-high-probability-short-term-trading-strategies-4m242dmrshc0)). The long-side rules are as follows; shorts mirror them.

**Turtle Soup** buys a failed breakdown. Today makes a new 20-day low, and the previous 20-day low is at least four sessions old. A buy stop goes 5–10 ticks above that prior low, good for today only. The protective stop goes one tick under today's low and is then trailed, with one re-entry allowed.

**Turtle Soup Plus One** waits for a day-1 close at or below the prior 20-bar low, which must be at least three sessions old. On day 2 it buys with a stop order at that prior low. The protective stop goes one tick under the lower of the two days' lows, and partial profits come within 2–6 bars.

**80-20s** is a day trade. Yesterday must have opened in the top 20% of its range and closed in the bottom 20%. Today, after price trades below yesterday's low, a buy stop goes at yesterday's low, with the protective stop near today's low.

**Momentum Pinball** computes a 3-period RSI of the one-day price change. When it is below 30, the next day's trade is a buy stop above the first-hour high, with a stop at the first-hour low.

**Holy Grail** requires a 14-period ADX above 30 and rising, then waits for a pullback to the **20-period EMA**. Many web summaries wrongly use the simple moving average. It buys with a stop above the prior bar's high, places the protective stop at the new swing low and targets the recent swing high.

**ID/NR4** brackets an inside day that is also the narrowest range of the last four days. Stops go one tick on either side, the position reverses once if the first fill fails, and the trade exits on the close if not profitable within two days.

The book's money management enters full size at once and scales out. It gives no percentage risk rule.

Brandt trades classical chart-pattern breakouts on daily and weekly charts. He has narrowed to patterns with **horizontal boundaries**, such as rectangles and ascending or descending triangles, which he says are "generally more reliable than patterns breaking out of a diagonal boundary" ([Brandt on X, 2021](https://twitter.com/peterlbrandt/status/1397183251051995138?lang=en)). His **Last Day Rule** stop is "the low of the day of the breakout as long as at least half of the bar is below the boundary line. Otherwise... the low of the last full day within the pattern." He now often tightens it to the breakout day's open.

His target is a measured move. Once 70% of the target is reached he switches to a **3-Day Trailing Stop Rule**: find the high day of the move, wait for a setup day that closes below the high day's low, and exit when the setup day's low is broken ([Brandt Q&A and *Diary* pages](https://peterlbrandt.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/3-Day-Trailing-Stop-Rule-3DTSR.pdf)). In his words: "Target always. 3-day trailing stop rule once 70% to target has been achieved" ([Brandt on X, 2018](https://x.com/peterlbrandt/status/961052548021940224)).

A review of his chapter in *Unknown Market Wizards* adds more detail. He risks about 0.5% per trade and moves stops to breakeven within 2–3 days. He takes at most one re-entry and liquidates open losers at Friday's close. He also holds that classical charting has lost much of its edge ([Smart-Lab review](https://smart-lab.ru/blog/reviews/889963.php)). Deciding what counts as a "textbook" rectangle remains his judgment.

Cameron's record is both the most heavily documented and the most contested in the group, because two authorities examined two different things. The first is a CPA examination. SingerLewak LLP's report, dated March 17, 2026, says the firm confirmed balances and transactions directly with the brokers for July 2020–December 2025. For 2017 to mid-2020 it relies on another firm's unmodified 2020 opinion, and it is "intended solely for the information and use of Ross Cameron" ([SingerLewak report p.1](https://media.warriortrading.com/2026/03/26191855/Ross-Cameron-12.31.25-InvestmentPortfolio-1.jpg)). The schedule totals **$18,810,638 in realized gains** from a $583.15 start, with yearly figures from $309,884 (2023) to $6,200,647 (2025) ([schedule p.2](https://media.warriortrading.com/2026/03/26191900/Ross-Cameron-12.31.25-InvestmentPortfolio-2.jpg)). Its notes exclude brokerage fees and do not count unrealized gains or losses ([notes p.7](https://media.warriortrading.com/2026/03/26191918/Ross-Cameron-12.31.25-InvestmentPortfolio-7.jpg)). Large withdrawals, $7.5M in 2025 alone, leave small balances at the start of each month. That makes headline percentages such as a "61% average monthly return" nearly meaningless; the dollar figure is the meaningful one.

At least one marketing claim does not match the schedule on its face. Warrior says the account "grew to over $100,000" within 45 days ([Warrior, Day Trading guide](https://www.warriortrading.com/day-trading/)). ## Independent evidence backs the ingredients, not the patterns

| Setup family | Best independent evidence | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| ICT / smart-money concepts | 44 detectors on S&P 500 daily bars; 6 rules in intraday G10 FX; 648 ETF daily backtests | No edge over random entry after correction; sweeps lean toward continuation; 0 of 648 beat buy-and-hold |
| 5-min ORB on QQQ | Zarattini & Aziz 2023; two independent replications | About +0.13R gross; roughly zero after 2¢/share of slippage |
| Intraday trend (noise area, VWAP) | Author papers; GitHub replication; Gao et al. 2018 | Replicates through 2024; Sharpe near zero since 2025 |
| Small-cap gap momentum | Berkman et al. 2012; Robinhood herding study | High-attention gappers tend to reverse intraday; Cameron's filter is untested |
| Momentum breakouts (Qullamaggie, Minervini, Kell) | Jegadeesh-Titman 1993; George-Hwang 2004; Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen 2012 | Ingredients supported; specific patterns untested; crash risk; earnings drift near zero after 2017 |
| Classical patterns / *Street Smarts* | Lo et al. 2000; Savin et al. 2007; Marshall et al. 2008; McLean-Pontiff 2016 | Some information, little profit after costs; none of 7,846 five-minute SPY rules survives correction for data snooping |

The independent record on smart-money concepts is null to negative. None of it is peer-reviewed, and none of it tests the 1–5-minute index-futures setting the concepts are taught for. A September 2026 SSRN working paper turned two open-source Pine Script implementations into **44 detectors** and tested them on 496 S&P 500 stocks from 2010 to mid-2026, comparing each trade against the stock's own unconditional return. After a Benjamini-Hochberg correction across 352 hypotheses, "none beats random entry at any horizon." Concepts selected on three years of data earned −31 basis points out of sample ([Tangirala, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7483658)).

A second SSRN paper tested six ICT rules in intraday G10 FX. It found no support for the rules as taught, and found that **swept levels tend to be followed by continuation, not reversal** ([Mahadzva, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7430998)). That fits New York Fed work showing that stop-loss orders cluster just beyond round numbers, and that trends become "unusually rapid" when those levels are hit, a cascade mechanism ([Osler, NY Fed SR 150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)). A practitioner study of SPY, QQQ, DIA and IWM daily bars found **0 of 648 ICT backtests beat buy-and-hold**, and only one of 32 t-statistics exceeded 2 ([StatOasis](https://statoasis.com/overfit/research/ict-backtest-what-survives)). The one transparent mechanical Silver Bullet test on NQ/ES 5-minute bars shows 37 NQ trades with a 29.7% win rate and a 0.61 profit factor. Its author calls the sample "not statistically meaningful" ([GitHub silver-bullet-backtest](https://github.com/cjosh4toyotas-stack/silver-bullet-backtest)).

The research ORB grew $25,000 to $192,806 on QQQ from January 2016 to February 2023. That is a 675% return against 169% for buy-and-hold, with a Sharpe ratio of 1.12, a 24% win rate and an average of +0.13R over 1,795 trades. The test assumed no slippage and a $0.0005/share commission ([Zarattini & Aziz 2023](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5983d931579fb366729580d8/t/643ed6765176b45506e41a01/1681839734183/SSRN-id4416622.pdf)).

An independent GitHub replication reproduced the result "within noise," with 1,775 trades and a Sharpe of 1.06. But the gross edge was only **7 cents per share**, and net profit crossed zero at **about 2.2 cents per share of slippage**. With 2 cents of entry slippage and 4 cents of stop slippage, the Sharpe ratio fell to 0.23, against 0.72 for simply holding QQQ. The 10R target was hit on only 2–3% of trades, and 2022 alone supplied 38% of profits ([giovannibrusco replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)). A second replication on index CFDs from 2015 to mid-2026 reproduced the gross edge on Nasdaq (+0.131R, t = 2.9). After spread and slippage, though, it found **+0.002R**, negative results in 2015–2017 in every market, and at most +0.06R on Nasdaq from 2021 to 2026 ([Krueger, MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)).

The broader academic picture matches. A 2008 study of **7,846 intraday rules on 5-minute SPY data found none significant after correction for data snooping**; channel breakouts were the most profitable family before that correction ([Marshall, Cahan & Cahan 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v15y2008i2p199-210.html)). Zarattini, Aziz and Barbon's intraday trend strategy on SPY returned 1,985% in total from 2007 to early 2024, with a Sharpe ratio of 1.33 ([SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/fr/publications/n-24-97-beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-s-p500-etf-spy)). It replicated at a Sharpe of 1.11 for 2020–2026, but the replicator reports a Sharpe near zero since 2025 ([giovannibrusco SPY replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy)). The most durable intraday result is market-level: SPY's return from the prior close to 10:00 predicts its 15:30–16:00 return, especially on volatile, high-volume and news days ([Gao et al. 2018](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/)).

For Cameron's universe, the evidence leans against the naive trade. High-attention stocks tend to post positive overnight returns followed by intraday reversals, a hidden cost of buying at the open ([Berkman et al. 2012](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/paying-attention-overnight-returns-and-the-hidden-cost-of-buying-at-the-open/F9AAD159B512C651F09D5D52011D88E0)). Intense herding by Robinhood users is followed by negative abnormal returns ([Barber et al.](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/SSRN-id3715077.pdf)). No peer-reviewed study tests Cameron's exact filter net of costs. His explanation of where his profits come from, relative volume of 5× and up in low-float names, cannot be reproduced on QQQ, SPY or mega-caps.

The swing traders' ingredients have the strongest academic footing. Past 3–12-month winners outperform past losers; the best 12-month/3-month strategy earned 1.31% a month in 1965–1989 ([Jegadeesh & Titman 1993](https://floridapsc.com/library/filings/2013/07457-2013/Support/Gulf's%20response%20to%20OPC's%201st%20POD,%20No.%2057/Jegadeesh%20and%20Titman%201993%20(140-167).pdf)). Nearness to the 52-week high explains much of momentum's profit without a long-run reversal ([George & Hwang 2004](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/361244)), which is close to the Trend Template's "within 25% of the high" rule. Time-series momentum appears in all 58 liquid futures markets studied ([Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463)), supporting MA-trend filters and trailing exits.

These ingredients carry known costs. Momentum crashes after market panics, when past losers rebound sharply ([Daniel & Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)). Post-earnings drift, the academic cousin of the episodic pivot, has decayed to "indistinguishable from zero" after 2017 ([Kettell et al. 2022](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/CEASA/Events%20Page/PEAD_Declined_over_time.pdf)). Drift after news, alongside reversal after moves without news ([Chan 2003](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/28248)), supports Qullamaggie's insistence on a news catalyst. No rigorous, survivorship-free backtest of the VCP, Qullamaggie's breakouts or Kell's cycle was found.

Classical patterns carry statistical information but little stand-alone profit. Automated pattern recognition on US stocks from 1962 to 1996 found that "several technical indicators do provide incremental information" ([Lo, Mamaysky & Wang 2000](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7613)). Head-and-shoulders showed "little or no support" on its own, though strategies conditioned on the pattern added 5–7% a year on a risk-adjusted basis ([Savin, Weller & Zvingelis 2007](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jfinec/v5yi2p243-265.html)). Bulkowski's self-published counts show patterns now fail 2–4× more often than in the past ([ThePatternSite](https://thepatternsite.com/FailureRates.html)).

When thousands of technical rules are tested together, the best in-sample rule had no economic value out of sample from 1987 to 1996 ([Sullivan, Timmermann & White 1999](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-11/dp303.pdf)). Apparent outperformance on the Dow from 1897 to 2011 was "completely offset by the introduction of low transaction costs" ([Bajgrowicz & Scaillet 2012](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v106y2012i3p473-491.html)). Published anomalies earn about **58% less after publication** ([McLean & Pontiff 2016](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)), which matters for setups printed in 1990 and 1995. No independent test of Turtle Soup on US stocks, or of the Holy Grail on any market, was found.

Base rates for people who adopt these styles are poor and consistent. In Brazil, **97% of people who day traded index futures for more than 300 days lost money**, and only 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage ([Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). In Taiwan, day traders' aggregate performance after fees was negative in every one of 15 years, and more than 75% quit within two years ([Barber et al. 2017](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)). Retail options buyers favor cheap weekly contracts with an **average bid-ask spread of 12.6%** and lose money on average ([Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya 2023](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827)). About 60% of retail losses on same-day S&P options came from transaction costs ([Swedroe on Beckmeyer et al.](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options)).

## Sixty sessions can test mechanics, not edges

### Sixty trades cannot separate +0.13R from zero

My own calculation, using the replication's payoff shape, shows the size of the problem. About 75% of trades stop out at −1R, about 2–3% reach +10R, and the rest close flat at the bell, which gives a per-trade standard deviation of roughly 2R. With 60 trades, the standard error of the average is about 0.26R, twice the +0.13R edge being sought. Reaching a t-statistic near 2 takes on the order of 1,000 trades, which fits the original study needing 1,795 trades over seven years. At a 24% win rate, 60 trades should also contain a run of **about ten consecutive losses** by chance alone. Aziz's own deck lists the TQQQ version at "24% win, 76% loss" ([BBT 5-Min ORB deck](https://bearbulltraders.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/5-Min-ORB-Strategy-ORB.pdf)).

This repository's earlier ORB variants (`reports/backtest_report.md`) show the problem directly. Over 40 test sessions they produced only 10–20 trades each, with 95% confidence intervals on the average R spanning roughly −1R to +1R. Pooling across mega-caps multiplies the trade count but not the information. These stocks move together on the same days, so the effective sample is closer to the number of sessions than to the number of symbol-days, and standard errors should be clustered by date.

The 60 sessions are well suited to four jobs. They can confirm that the code fires where a human would, check the fill logic, measure how sensitive results are to costs, and run high-frequency event studies, such as which way price goes after a sweep, that produce hundreds of events.

### Rules that run natively on 5-minute RTH bars

The specifications below share a set of conventions. Times are Eastern, and each bar is labelled by its start time, so the 09:30 bar covers 09:30–09:35. Only regular-hours data is used, and every level comes from RTH bars. ATR(14) is computed on daily bars unless stated otherwise, and VWAP is the cumulative typical price × volume from 09:30. Each symbol holds one position at a time. If a single bar touches both the stop and the target, it counts as a stop. Parameters marked "my choice" are mine, not the trader's.

M1, M2 and M4 are faithful to published rules. M3, M5, M7 and M8 are operationalizations that fill gaps the educators left open, so their results test the closest mechanical cousin, not the trader's discretionary practice. M6 is the most informative test the 60 sessions allow, for two reasons: sweeps are frequent, and the existing evidence says the ICT premise (that a sweep leads to a reversal) may be backwards.

A simple academic benchmark belongs beside these. Go long or short SPY/QQQ at the 15:30 bar's open, in the direction of the return from the prior close to 10:00, and exit at the 15:55 close ([Gao et al. 2018](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/)). Aziz's RSI-extreme reversal (RSI above 90 or below 10 after four or more same-coloured bars) will rarely trigger on SPY or QQQ, so it does not merit a slot.

### Rules for two years of daily bars

Two years is about 500 bars, and long lookbacks eat that history quickly. The Trend Template's 52-week rules need 252 bars of warm-up, leaving only about 250 testable bars, and Qullamaggie's 6-month ranking leaves about 380.

Several of these rules fit QQQ, SPY and mega-caps poorly. D5 and D6 are built for high-volatility leaders: a third-party implementation of Qullamaggie's scan treats an average daily range of roughly 5–6% as the mark of a "momentum leader" ([TradingView ADR script](https://www.tradingview.com/script/ZBI3bJ2W-Qullamaggie-Daily-with-ADR-and-Compression-Range)). Most mega-caps sit well below that, so expect few signals. D7 will find only a handful of 8-week-plus patterns per symbol in two years. Relative strength ranked within a small universe is not IBD's full-market rank. And a mega-cap list chosen today carries survivorship bias, so the universe should be fixed as of the test's start date.

### Hygiene that keeps a small test honest

A small test stays honest only if **every parameter is fixed before the first run**, using the book's values where they exist and never re-tuning after seeing results. Costs and fills should be modelled explicitly. That means the replication's 2¢ entry slippage and 4¢ stop slippage on QQQ and SPY, gap fills at the open rather than at the stop price, and a stop whenever one bar touches both the stop and the target. Each rule should report expectancy in R with a 95% confidence interval clustered by date. It should also report win rate, payoff ratio, maximum drawdown and trade count by market regime, because 2022-style trend years dominated both ORB studies, and it should be compared against the random-entry and buy-and-hold benchmarks. Each extra variant raises the odds of a lucky winner, so a **Benjamini-Hochberg correction across all variants** belongs in the write-up, as in the smart-money-concepts paper ([Tangirala, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7483658)). The last 15–20 sessions should stay untouched until the rules are frozen.

The same discipline applies to replaying historical charts by eye. Picking setups after the fact from many charts is the data-snooping problem in miniature. Writing the rule down first, then logging every qualifying chart, winners and losers alike, turns practice into evidence.

### What a $60 options account changes

None of these traders' sizing rules survives a $60 account. A 1% risk is 60 cents and Qullamaggie's 0.25% is 15 cents, so a single option contract puts a large fraction of the account, or all of it, on one trade. Costs also move from secondary to decisive. As an illustration, paying half of a 12.6% average spread on a $1.00, 0.5-delta contract costs about 6 cents of premium. That is equivalent to roughly 12 cents of QQQ movement per side, several times the 2.2 cents per share that erased the ORB's edge in the replication. The 12.6% figure is an average across retail option trades, not a QQQ-specific measurement, so the quoted spreads on your own fills are worth recording. The practical sequence is to test each rule on the underlying in R, log option fills separately to see how much of the underlying's edge survives the spread, and treat a small live account as a way to measure execution rather than as a test of the strategy.

## Conclusion

The strongest verification in this group mostly establishes something other than what a follower needs. Cameron's broker-confirmed P&L and Minervini's and Kell's contest wins are real. Yet the one regulator that examined customer outcomes for a famous educator alleged that most of those customers lost money: the audited dollars measure the teacher, not whether the method transfers. The evidence also cuts against the most popular framework here. The best intraday test of the smart-money idea found that swept levels tend to continue rather than reverse, which is closer to the breakout you already trade than to an ICT reversal. A sweep-direction event study on your own 5-minute data can check that claim cheaply, because sweeps are frequent enough to produce a real sample.

The setup you already trade is the best-documented strategy in this whole field. Its fate is decided less by the entry signal than by spread, slippage and a handful of trend days. Whether a small account can capture an edge that thin depends on lowering cost per trade and on collecting many more trades, not on finding a better entry. That points to a practical sequence. Lengthen the 5-minute history toward years, freeze one rule set, measure in R on the underlying, and treat every famous trader's setup as a hypothesis with a pre-registered specification rather than a track record to inherit.
